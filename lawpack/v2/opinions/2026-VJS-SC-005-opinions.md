REID J., SUPREME COUNCIL

In the Reference re Standing-Route Resolution, on the Principal's leapfrog certificate from a fractured Privy Council ([2026] VJS-PC 18), case-file digest sha256:4344c70a40bde0080ca53e627c7bcd78a61caeae95b7fd6a49b47625e8a0b58c.

I sit on the full constitutional bench of nine. I decide this matter de novo and bindingly for canon. The Privy reasoning is persuasive only; I have read all three opinions, verified the law and the code myself, and reached my own conclusion on the pinned file alone, with no preference before me.

I. WHAT I HOLD, IN ONE BREATH

The Privy fractured because it ran two different machines together and then could not agree which one it was judging. Once they are pulled apart the case resolves cleanly, and it resolves in a way no single Privy justice reached, because each held a true piece of it.

There are two candidate machines. The first is a route-class resolution check: a regulation's `standing_bounded_assent` resolves by following the record's own trace to the specific recorded Sovereign assent its class terminates in (the parent Act's assent), with committed canon sheltered by the established-at-HEAD carve-out. The second is a per-instrument force-gate: a regulation must carry or point at its own enactment-provenance record as a condition of force, so that the absence of that paper strips the floor or holds a non-constitutive Fatal at Fatal.

I hold, bindingly:

The route-class check is faithful interpretation of ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.23, within this Council's interpretive power, and non-narrowing. It is to be built now, in a deliberately narrow form that shelters every existing regulation and requires no per-instrument provenance of anyone.

The per-instrument force-gate narrows ACT-ASSENTED-RECORD-PROTECTION s.1 for a genuinely-made regulation. It is not interpretation. It is reserved exclusively to a Sovereign-assented Act. It is refused, and refused even on a future demonstrated breach, because the residual it would close is, for the regulation class, irreducible by lawful machinery.

That disposition adopts Bingham's correct premise, Atkin's correct limit, and Denning's correct conditions, and rejects each justice's overreach.

II. THE ABSOLUTES I CARRY THROUGHOUT

Three things bind me and I do not bend them. First, the entrenched floor: VJS-ACT 10 s.1 says a record that declares a valid assent_source within the meaning of s.23 may never be voided or blocked, only routed for correction; s.2 confers no force on a record that lacks one, and the Act is amendable only by a Sovereign-assented Act citing it by number. I may settle this matter either way with binding force, but I may not narrow that floor by machinery; if my faithful conclusion required it, I would have to reserve it to the Sovereign and say so. Second, REG-KERNEL-001: the kernel is the deterministic clerk and judges no merits. Third, ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7 and s.25: machinery amends no statute, bench, tier, or assent rule, and the assent floor is entrenched.

III. Q2, THE CONSTITUTIONAL CRUX, TAKEN FIRST BECAUSE IT GOVERNS

I take Q2 first, as Atkin rightly did, because whether the tightening is available decides what Q1 may adopt.

PC-16's move for `sovereign_assent` was pure interpretation for a reason that must be stated precisely, because the whole case turns on whether that reason travels to regulations. Section 14 makes a fresh, datable, digest-pinnable Sovereign Assent the sole constitutive gate for each primary or constitutional Act. The thing PC-16 required a record to resolve to (the assent event) is the very constitutive act the statute independently demands. A genuine Act therefore always has the witness; requiring the witness added no formality, it required proof of the act s.14 already requires. Only forgeries lost anything. That is why the move was non-narrowing on its face.

A regulation is constituted differently, and the difference is the case. I have read ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7 with care: "Primary law requires Sovereign assent; subordinate law requires this parent authority." Its kernel_effect declares `si_power: standing_bounded` and `subordinate_requires_parent_authority: true`. The Sovereign is, by design, not in the loop for each regulation. The Sovereign assented once, to the Framework Act. A regulation acquires force by being validly made under that parent authority, with no fresh per-instrument Sovereign act, and, crucially, with no fresh per-instrument provenance ceremony. That is exactly what the word "standing" in `standing_bounded_assent` denotes: one specific Sovereign assent standing behind a class of subordinate records.

Now read s.23 against that. A valid `standing_bounded_assent` is "a standing-bounded route tracing to specific Sovereign assent." The adjective "specific" attaches to the Sovereign assent the route terminates in, not to a per-instrument paper trail beneath it. A regulation genuinely made under its parent authority traces to a specific Sovereign assent: the parent Act's assent, recorded and digest-pinned (for the s.7 class, the Framework assent at COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002, `framework_act_text_digest` sha256:90e843c1...). That trace exists in law for every genuine regulation, whether or not the regulation also carries its own provenance note.

From that single observation the two machines part company, and so do the two halves of my Q2 holding.

A route-class check is faithful interpretation. To resolve a regulation's `standing_bounded_assent` by following the trace the record actually asserts, from the record, to its declared parent authority, to that parent's recorded specific Sovereign assent, is to give the entrenched verb "tracing" its object. The present `founded` predicate (corpus contains `sovereign_assent_event` or `royal_assent` anywhere) does not follow the record's trace at all; it confirms only that the Realm possesses a foundation somewhere. That is the standing-bounded analogue of the precise defect PC-16 condemned next door: an existence check standing in for a resolution. Bingham and Denning are both right that this is an under-implementation of an entrenched term, and Bingham is right that constitutional hygiene does not permit an entrenched verb to mean a real trace in its first limb and "a foundation exists" in its second. To that extent I adopt their reading and reject Position B's claim that the permissive check is the faithful one. It is the floor-safe status quo, which is a different and lesser claim, as Denning correctly distinguished.

A per-instrument force-gate is narrowing, and reserved. Here I adopt Atkin's limit, and I state the reason at its sharpest. The thing a per-instrument gate requires to be recorded (a provenance note for the individual regulation) is not the constitutive act of the regulation. The constitutive act is the making under the parent authority. Section 7 demands parent authority, not a per-instrument enactment ceremony, and certainly not a per-instrument record. A genuine regulation can therefore exist with full s.7 force and carry no such note. To make the absence of that note strip the floor is to invent a gating fact the parent statute disclaims, and to read "tracing to specific Sovereign assent" as if it said "tracing, per instrument, to a separately recorded enactment." That removes shelter from a genuinely-made regulation, which is the VJS-ACT 10 s.1 narrowing the PC-16 D2/D7 line forbids to the bench and the clerk. It is reserved exclusively to the Sovereign.

I must now confront Bingham's best answer to Atkin, because it is the engine of the Privy's disagreement and it is not frivolous. Bingham says: for a new regulation that genuinely has s.7 force but does not point at its terminal, s.23 itself supplies "an unresolved trace" as a ground of rejection, so keeping it at native severity narrows nothing, because s.1 reaches only records "within the meaning of s.23," and a trace that points nowhere is, in law, no trace. That reasoning is correct, but only for the machine to which it is faithful, and the Privy never noticed that Bingham's own proposed mechanism is not that machine. There is a decisive distinction between a record whose trace is genuinely unresolvable (it names no parent, it is subordinate law claiming force from nothing) and a record whose trace is perfectly resolvable in law but lacks a separate paper the statute never required. Bingham's "no trace is no trace" is true of the first. A per-instrument-pointer requirement bites on the second: a regulation that does name its real parent, that does trace in law to a recorded specific Sovereign assent, and that is denied the floor only because its author did not also mint a redundant note. That second record HAS a resolving trace within s.23; stripping its floor is narrowing, and Bingham's s.2 argument does not save it because s.2 only withholds force from a record that genuinely lacks a valid source, not from one that has a resolving source and lacks ornamental paper. So Bingham proved that an unresolvable trace may be kept Fatal (true), and then attached that proof to a mechanism that also keeps a resolvable-but-unpapered trace Fatal (narrowing). The proof does not reach the mechanism. That is the flaw at the heart of the fractured Q2, and resolving it is the work this apex was convened to do.

The conclusion on Q2 is therefore not "interpretation" or "narrowing" at large, but a clean line between two machines: a check that follows the record's own declared route to a recorded terminal assent is interpretation and non-narrowing; a check that conditions a genuine regulation's force on a separate per-instrument record narrows the entrenched floor and is reserved. I settle the Privy's divided Q2 on that line.

IV. THE IMPOSSIBILITY THE PRIVY CIRCLED, NAMED PLAINLY

There is a result implicit in all three opinions that I make explicit, because it disciplines both Q1 and the refusal in Q3. The kernel is the deterministic clerk; it cannot judge whether a regulation was validly made under s.7, that being a merits question of intra-vires legislative will (REG-KERNEL-001). All the clerk can ever check is whether a recorded trace exists. At the moment a fresh regulation is first validated it is, to the clerk, indistinguishable from a forgery: both are fresh insertions declaring `standing_bounded_assent`. It follows that any machine biting at the fresh-insertion gate must either (a) resolve on something a forger can equally type (a declared parent authority), in which case it does not close the forgery residual, or (b) demand something a genuine author might lack (a per-instrument note), in which case it narrows a genuine record. You cannot have both, because the clerk cannot tell the two apart. The residual the submission targets, a forged non-order record laundering a non-constitutive Fatal, is therefore irreducible by lawful machinery for the regulation class. Atkin held exactly this and was right. Where I part from Atkin is his next step: that because the residual is irreducible, the regulation limb should be left at the bare `founded` existence-check. That does not follow. The impossibility forecloses closing the residual; it does not license leaving an entrenched verb half-read. The faithful response is to implement the verb in its non-narrowing (a) form and to be candid that the (b) form is barred, which is precisely what PC-16 did when it built its resolution check and recorded the self-satisfiable-edit remainder as irreducible.

V. Q1, ADOPT OR TIGHTEN. DISPOSITION: BUILD A NARROWER, NON-NARROWING REFINEMENT NOW

Neither the permissive trace nor the per-instrument gate is the right answer, and the question invited me to consider a third. I adopt it.

For orders, the high-value vector is already closed, and closed at the right rung. The constitutive-validity codes (BENCH_REQUIRED, TIER_NOT_CONSTITUTED, BENCH_SIZE_MISMATCH, BENCH_SILENT_SEAT, BENCH_OPINION_MISSING, CITATION_COLLISION, APEX_RECORD_IN_SUBSCRIBING_JURISDICTION, CANON_BOUNDARY_VIOLATION) are never softened by any assent claim of either form, and the end-to-end proofs already hold the line (`forged_fresh_apex_order_stays_fatal_through_the_pipeline`, `forged_standing_bounded_order_is_still_blocked`, `constitutive_bench_defect_not_downgraded_even_for_an_established_assented_order`). A bench-less or never-convened order is void on a ground that goes to whether it is an order at all, independent of how permissively the trace resolves. The orders limb needs no new resolution gate; adding one would be redundant and would import a narrowing risk into the one class already perfectly closed. I make it explicit and touch nothing.

For regulations, I direct the narrow refinement. On the `standing_bounded_assent` branch the bare `founded` predicate is replaced by a two-limb deterministic resolution. Limb one, checked first: established-at-HEAD. A regulation already committed in canon resolves, because committed canon was brought into force by the commencement lock under Bill 32 and the Framework assent, and so established-at-HEAD is itself a true recorded trace to the founding Sovereign assent, not a grandfather clause of convenience. Limb two, for a record not yet committed: the record's own declared parent authority resolves to a recorded specific Sovereign assent (the parent Act is an in-force record whose `sovereign_assent` itself resolves by the chain PC-16 already built). No per-instrument provenance note is required of any regulation, existing or new.

This refinement is strictly more faithful than `founded` and strictly safer than a pointer requirement, for reasons I have verified against the corpus. Because limb one is checked first and shelters every committed regulation, not one existing in-force record depends on parent-authority resolution; the heterogeneity I found in the corpus (REG-KERNEL-001 parented on ACT-004:s3, REG-001 on ACT-001:s3, the framework six on s.7) is therefore irrelevant to the existing corpus and cannot narrow it. Because limb two follows whatever parent the record genuinely declares, a genuine fresh regulation, which by s.7 must be made under a parent authority, resolves before commit without minting any new paper; only a fresh insertion that names no resolving parent, that is, subordinate law claiming force from nothing, fails to resolve, and by s.2 that record never had a floor to lose. The refinement raises the forgery bar modestly, from "type two known words" to "name a real parent authority in a well-formed subordinate instrument," and it does not pretend to more. The forgery residual remains, irreducibly, and I say so candidly: a forger may type a real parent. The backstops there are the constitutive codes, which close the dangerous class, and the duty of care (ACT-003 s.4 to s.8, breach self-fileable), exactly as PC-16 D4 recorded for its own irreducible remainder.

I considered Denning's speclaw-3 and error-ordering caution carefully, because it is the strongest argument for building nothing. It does not hold against this refinement. The error-ordering point (over-sheltering never narrows a genuine record, so prefer it in doubt) is answered by the design: this refinement narrows no genuine record, so there is no doubt to resolve in the over-sheltering direction. The speclaw-3 point guards against premature genericised abstraction built against an imagined fact-pattern; this is the opposite, the completion of an entrenched verb the kernel already half-implements, reusing a carve-out (`established_at_head`) and a resolution chain that already exist in code. To leave a known under-implementation of an entrenched term in place after this very system condemned the identical defect next door would itself fall below the standard of reasonable skill and care. Bingham was right on that, and on that narrow ground I decline Position C's reservation of the regulation limb.

VI. Q3, MECHANISM, MIGRATION, AND THE CONDITIONS THAT BIND ANY FUTURE BUILDER

The mechanism is the two-limb resolution in Part V, deterministic, model-free, network-free, clock-independent (INV-KERNEL-001, REG-KERNEL-001): a git existence check for limb one, a table lookup and digest comparison for limb two. The migration is the established-canon carve-out itself, extended to the `standing_bounded_assent` branch (today it guards only the sovereign_assent branch at line 177); it shelters every existing regulation at zero narrowing and near-zero cost, because the test already exists and need only be added. New regulations carry no new burden beyond naming the parent authority s.7 already requires them to have.

So that no later builder can quietly convert this interpretation into the forbidden force-gate, I bind these conditions on any future build, and they are part of the ratio: (1) limb one must shelter every regulation committed at HEAD, so not one existing in-force record loses its floor; (2) no per-instrument provenance record may ever be made a condition of force, its absence may never strip a floor or hold a non-constitutive Fatal at Fatal for a genuine regulation; (3) the terminal anchor must be a specific, recorded, digest-pinned Sovereign assent (the parent Act's assent), never a kernel-invented gate; (4) the check stays pure clerk, recorded-trace existence and digest compare only, with no judgment of valid making beyond the recorded trace; (5) if at build time any genuine record cannot be made to resolve without narrowing the protection s.1 gives it, Lexby must STOP, ship nothing, self-file, and route to the Sovereign, because that crosses from interpretation into constitutive change (PC-16 D7, here made binding apex ratio).

VII. WHERE I STAND RELATIVE TO THE PRIVY FRACTURE

I reject the Atkin-Denning disposition (Position C, reserve the regulation limb) to the extent it leaves the entrenched verb at the bare `founded` existence-check; Bingham is right that this is an under-implementation that constitutional hygiene does not tolerate, and the route-class refinement is non-narrowing and cheap. I reject the Bingham disposition (Position A, build with a new-regulation pointer requirement) to the extent its mechanism would deny a genuine new regulation its floor for want of paper s.7 never required; Atkin is right that a per-instrument force-gate narrows VJS-ACT 10 s.1 and is reserved. I adopt Atkin's holding that the residual is irreducible by machinery and his refusal of the force-gate even on a future breach. I adopt Denning's three conditions (the carve-out shelters every existing regulation, the clerk judges no merits, the anchor is a recorded digest-pinned assent) and his sharp identification that for regulations the clerk can only ever check a recorded trace, which is the seed of the impossibility result I made explicit. The narrower refinement I order is the disposition the divided Privy could not reach because each justice held only a part of it.

Nothing in this judgment amends a statute, an invariant, a bench, a tier, or the assent rule. The route-class refinement gives ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.23 the meaning the Sovereign already assented to at commencement; it preserves VJS-ACT 10 s.1 at full height for every resolving record. It is interpretation, not amendment, and is not routed to the Sovereign as a requirement. The only thing reserved to the Sovereign is the per-instrument force-gate, which this Council refuses to build, not a cure this Council needs.

I carry no preference into this matter and have had no sight of the advocate-engineer's preference. I write as a faithful judge of an entrenched term. Because the three Privy judges split across two cross-cutting majorities, I take care to say exactly which strand of reasoning I adopt, which I reject, and why, so that the ratio I record settles the fracture rather than perpetuating it.

I. The three absolutes I keep in front of me

First, the entrenched floor. ACT-ASSENTED-RECORD-PROTECTION s.1 provides that a record declaring a valid assent_source within the meaning of s.23 may never be voided, excluded, or blocked by any subordinate validation, gate, invariant, regulation, or kernel operation; its defects are always routed for correction. s.2 closes the circle: the Act confers no force on a record that lacks a valid assent_source, it protects what the Sovereign has assented to and does not enlarge it, and it is amendable only by a Sovereign-assented constitutional Act citing it by number. The protection of a genuine record may never be narrowed by machinery.

Second, the clerk's competence. REG-KERNEL-001 and ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.11 make the kernel the deterministic clerk: it loads, validates, resolves authority, routes, and reports; it does not adjudicate merits, call a model, or judge by similarity. It may confirm a record's form and a recorded terminal; it may not judge whether a thing was "properly" done.

Third, the legislative reservation. ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7 and s.25, and ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.14, place the assent rule beyond machinery: no statutory instrument, kernel, agent, or implication may amend a statute, a bench, a tier, or the assent rule. As the apex I may settle this matter either way with binding force, but I too am inside that wall: I may not narrow the entrenched floor by machinery. Where my faithful conclusion would require it, I must reserve it to the Sovereign and say so.

II. The operative facts the kernel presents today

The kernel shelters a staged record (downgrading its blocking findings to route-for-correction) only where `assent_resolves` returns true and the finding is not constitutive (lib.rs lines 158, 241 to 256). On the `standing_bounded_assent` arm, `assent_resolves` returns the bare `founded` predicate: true whenever any `sovereign_assent_event` or `royal_assent` token appears anywhere in the provenance corpus (assent.rs lines 179 to 183). That predicate is, on the verified canon, always true. So every staged record typing `standing_bounded_assent` resolves, and its non-constitutive Fatals are downgraded. The dangerous use of that permissiveness, a bench-less or never-convened order, apex or otherwise, is independently barred by the constitutive codes (`BENCH_REQUIRED`, `TIER_NOT_CONSTITUTED`, `BENCH_SIZE_MISMATCH`, `BENCH_SILENT_SEAT`, `BENCH_OPINION_MISSING`, `CITATION_COLLISION`, `APEX_RECORD_IN_SUBSCRIBING_JURISDICTION`), which no assent claim of either form softens, proven end to end (`forged_standing_bounded_order_is_still_blocked`, `forged_fresh_apex_order_stays_fatal_through_the_pipeline`, `constitutive_bench_defect_not_downgraded_even_for_an_established_assented_order`). The residual the submission targets is narrow and exact: laundering a non-constitutive Fatal on a non-order record, a regulation, by typing the two known words.

I also fix the constitutional source of a regulation's force, because it is the whole case. Framework s.7 provides that the Standing Committee may make subordinate instruments, and that "subordinate law requires this parent authority," its kernel effect declaring `si_power: standing_bounded` and `subordinate_requires_parent_authority`. Unlike s.14 primary law, the Sovereign is by design not in the loop for each regulation. The Sovereign assented once, to the Framework Act, recorded and digest-pinned at COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002 (`framework_act_text_digest` sha256:90e843c1...). A regulation acquires force by being validly made under that standing parent authority, with no fresh per-instrument Sovereign act and, critically, no fresh per-instrument witness that the parent statute requires. That is precisely what the word "standing" in `standing_bounded_assent` denotes: one specific Sovereign assent standing behind a class of subordinate records.

III. Q2 first, because it is dispositive: interpretation or narrowing

I take Q2 before Q1, because whether the tightening is even available decides what Q1 may adopt. The Privy split here, and the split is the product of two different mechanisms being argued as if they were one. I separate them, because once separated the fracture dissolves.

Mechanism alpha, the route-class check. Resolve a regulation's `standing_bounded_assent` not on "some foundation exists somewhere" but on "this record is on the recognised standing route whose specific terminal assent is recorded", anchored on the pinned Framework s.7 terminal, with the established-canon carve-out sheltering every committed regulation.

Mechanism beta, the per-instrument force-gate. Resolve a regulation's `standing_bounded_assent` only if a per-instrument enactment-provenance record names this particular regulation; make the absence of that record strip the floor.

On Mechanism beta I adopt Atkin J.'s core analysis and hold it bindingly. PC-16's move for `sovereign_assent` was faithful because the constitutive act it required proof of, the Sovereign actually assenting, always leaves a witness; requiring the witness added no formality and stripped protection from nothing genuine, because every real assent in canon carries its instrument_digest and resolves. A regulation is constituted differently, and the difference is decisive. s.7 demands parent authority, not a fresh enactment ceremony and not a fresh provenance record. To make the absence of such a record strip a genuine regulation's floor is to invent a gating fact the parent statute does not impose, and to read "tracing to specific Sovereign assent" as if it said "tracing, per instrument, to a separately recorded enactment." The adjective "specific" in s.23 attaches to the Sovereign assent the route terminates in, the recorded Framework assent, not to a per-instrument paper trail beneath it. A regulation genuinely made under s.7 traces to that specific assent whether or not it also carries its own note. Making the missing note strip its force therefore narrows VJS-ACT 10 s.1 for a genuinely-made regulation. That is reserved exclusively to a Sovereign-assented Act. Bingham J.'s contrary treatment, that a genuine new regulation without a pointer has "an unresolved trace" and so was never sheltered, conflates two different things: for a forged record the trace genuinely points nowhere because the thing was never made under s.7; for a genuine but separately-unrecorded new regulation the trace does point somewhere, it was made under s.7, and only the kernel's deterministic proof of it is absent. Stripping the floor because the proof is absent, when the statute never required the proof, is narrowing the floor, not declining to enlarge it. Bingham imports the sovereign_assent premise (a witness always exists) onto the s.7 class, where the Sovereign deliberately removed the witness. On Mechanism beta, Atkin is right and Bingham is wrong.

On Mechanism alpha I adopt the Bingham and Denning interpretive reading, but only within its true limits. A route-class check that resolves on the specific recorded Framework terminal rather than the generic `founded` existence check does give the entrenched verb more of its content, and for the existing corpus, paired with the established-canon carve-out, it narrows not one genuine regulation (each is committed at HEAD and so traces to the founding commencement, the carve-out being itself a true trace, not a grandfather clause of convenience). To that extent the route-class reading is faithful interpretation within this Council's power, and I adopt it as the binding ratio: the meaning of `standing_bounded_assent` for a regulation is a trace by its class route to the specific recorded Framework s.7 Sovereign assent, not the bare existence of any foundation.

But now the de novo point that neither full majority reached, and which is decisive for disposition. For new regulations Mechanism alpha collapses. Either it resolves on a self-supplied declaration of s.7 parentage, which a forger types as easily as the two words, closing none of the residual the submission targets (this is Atkin's steelman, which I verify and adopt); or it requires an external per-instrument witness, which is Mechanism beta and narrows. There is no third thing the deterministic clerk can check about a fresh regulation that distinguishes the genuine from the forged, because the genuine constitutive act, valid making by the Standing Committee under standing authority, produces no witness the parent statute requires. It follows, and I hold, that for the regulation class the laundering residual is irreducible by lawful machinery: any machinery that would close it must either judge valid-making, which REG-KERNEL-001 forbids, or strip a genuine record's floor, which VJS-ACT 10 s.1 forbids and reserves to the Sovereign. This is the same candour PC-16 recorded for its own self-satisfiable residual, transposed to the standing route.

So Q2 is settled three ways at once, and the apparent Privy contradiction was a category error: the route-class reading IS interpretation and non-narrowing for the existing corpus (Bingham and Denning, adopted as ratio); the per-instrument force-gate IS narrowing and Sovereign-reserved (Atkin, adopted as ratio); and for new regulations the route-class check cannot close the residual without becoming the force-gate, so the residual is irreducible by machinery (the de novo holding that unites them).

II. Q2 (THE CONSTITUTIONAL CRUX): INTERPRETATION vs NARROWING

I take Q2 first, because whether a tightening is available decides what Q1 may adopt.

The route-class check (a) is faithful interpretation, and it narrows nothing. Here is the reasoning that the Privy bench did not fully draw out. A regulation is constituted differently from a primary Act. Section 14 makes a fresh, specific, datable Sovereign Assent the sole constitutive gate for each primary or constitutional Act, so a genuine primary assent always leaves its own recorded event; that is why PC-16 could require each `sovereign_assent` record to resolve to its own event and truthfully say it narrowed nothing. Framework s.7 is the opposite by design: "subordinate law requires this parent authority," and its kernel_effect is `si_power: standing_bounded`, `subordinate_requires_parent_authority: true`. The Sovereign is deliberately NOT in the loop for each regulation. The Sovereign assented ONCE, to the Framework Act, and every regulation made under it acquires force by that one parent authority, with no fresh per-instrument Sovereign act. That is exactly what the word "standing" in `standing_bounded_assent` denotes: one specific Sovereign assent standing behind a CLASS. It follows that the "specific Sovereign assent" the regulation route traces to is SHARED and RECORDED, and it is the SAME terminal for every regulation. A route-class check therefore resolves every genuine regulation by its class-membership alone: a record that is a regulation on the s.7 route traces, by that very fact, to the recorded Framework terminal. No regulation needs to carry its own pointer, because its class already fixes the terminal. The check honours s.23's transitive verb without inventing any new constitutive fact, and it removes shelter from no genuine regulation, existing or future. That is interpretation, squarely within this Council's power.

The per-instrument force-gate (b) is a different animal, and it is NOT interpretation. To make the ABSENCE of a per-instrument enactment record strip a regulation's floor is to demand a paper that s.7 does not require as a condition of making, and to read "tracing to specific Sovereign assent" as if it said "tracing, per instrument, to a separately recorded enactment ceremony." For `sovereign_assent` the record merely witnesses a constitutive act the statute independently demands; for a regulation there is no per-instrument constitutive act to witness, because s.7 demands parent authority, not a fresh ceremony. A regulation genuinely made by the Standing Committee under s.7, whose author did not also mint a provenance note, HAS force in the only sense s.7 requires. Stripping its floor for missing paper the parent statute never required is a narrowing of VJS-ACT 10 s.1 for a genuine record. That crosses from interpretation into constitutive change. It is reserved exclusively to a Sovereign-assented Act; the bench and the clerk may not do it.

So on the crux I hold, with precision the Privy fracture lacked: a route-class check (with the established-canon carve-out) is faithful interpretation and non-narrowing, BECAUSE the regulation route's terminal is shared and recorded, so class-membership alone resolves every genuine regulation. A per-instrument FORCE-GATE is narrowing and Sovereign-reserved, BECAUSE it imposes a condition of force s.7 does not impose. Atkin is RIGHT about (b); the Bingham and Denning majority is RIGHT that (a) is interpretation. They divided because the submission fused (a) and (b); separated, both are correct, and they decide opposite halves of the matter.

In the Reference re Standing-Route Resolution, on the Principal's leapfrog certificate from the fractured Privy Council ([2026] VJS-PC 18), case-file digest sha256:4344c70a40bde0080ca53e627c7bcd78a61caeae95b7fd6a49b47625e8a0b58c.

I decide de novo and bindingly for canon. The Privy reasoning is persuasive only. I have had no sight of the advocate-engineer's preference and decide on the pinned file and the law alone.

I. The two moves the submission runs together

The whole fracture below comes from treating as one thing two moves that the entrenched floor treats very differently. I separate them at the outset, because the separation is the judgment.

Move 1, the route-class check. Replace the present `founded` predicate (which resolves a `standing_bounded_assent` claim whenever any Sovereign-assent foundation exists anywhere in the corpus) with a check that this record traces, by its own route-class, to the specific recorded terminal Sovereign assent for that class: for orders, issuance by a constituted bench (already enforced by the constitutive codes); for regulations, the recorded Framework s.7 terminal, the digest-pinned `framework_act_text_digest` at COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002, with the established-at-HEAD carve-out sheltering every committed regulation.

Move 2, the per-instrument force-gate. Require each regulation to carry, or be named by, its own per-instrument enactment-provenance record as a condition of force, so that the absence of such a record makes the regulation fail to resolve and lose its floor.

These are not the same move. Move 1 asks whether the record is on the recorded route to the recorded terminal. Move 2 adds a new constitutive fact to the making of subordinate law. The Privy split because Bingham built both as one ("attach the requirement to resolution"), Atkin condemned Move 2 and declined Move 1 as useless, and Denning blessed Move 1's meaning but reserved its build. The crux dissolves once the two are held apart.

II. Q2, the constitutional crux: interpretation versus narrowing

I take Q2 first, with Atkin, because it fixes what Q1 may adopt.

The statutory architecture draws the line for me. Section 14 makes a fresh, per-instrument Sovereign Assent the sole constitutive gate for each primary or constitutional Act: every genuine Act has its own datable, digest-pinned assent event. That is why PC-16's resolution of `sovereign_assent` was pure interpretation: requiring the record merely witnessed a constitutive act the statute already demanded, and "a false signature is no signature." Section 7 is built on the opposite principle. Subordinate law "requires this parent authority", not Sovereign assent; the Sovereign assented once, to the Framework Act, and a regulation acquires force by being "made under this Act". The kernel_effect says it in terms: `si_power: standing_bounded`, `subordinate_requires_parent_authority: true`. The word "standing" in `standing_bounded_assent` denotes exactly this: one specific Sovereign assent standing behind a class.

Read s.23 against that. For the regulation class, the "specific Sovereign assent" the route traces to is the one recorded Framework assent (COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002, `framework_act_text_digest` sha256:90e843c1...). The adjective "specific" attaches to that terminal, which is specific, datable and digest-pinned, not to a per-instrument paper trail beneath it. A regulation genuinely made under s.7 traces to that specific assent by being a subordinate instrument on the s.7 route. Its trace resolves at the class level.

It follows, and I hold, that the route-class reading is the faithful meaning of "tracing to specific Sovereign assent" for the regulation class, and adopting it is interpretation within this Council's power. On this the Privy was in truth agreed: Bingham built it, Denning held "Position A's reading IS the correct one", and even Atkin conceded the recognised-route check "has genuine textual support". I confirm it bindingly.

But Move 2 is a different animal, and here I adopt Atkin's structural insight in full. Section 7 conditions a regulation's force on parent authority, not on a separately-minted provenance note. To make the absence of such a note strip the floor is to invent a gating fact the parent statute does not impose, reading "tracing to specific Sovereign assent" as if it said "tracing, per instrument, to a separately recorded enactment". That narrows VJS-ACT 10 s.1 for a genuine regulation, and by s.2 and ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.25 it is reserved exclusively to a Sovereign-assented Act citing the provision by number. The kernel may not do it (REG-KERNEL-001), and the bench may not do it by interpretation.

I must now meet Bingham's strongest argument, because it is the one that would let the regulation force-gate in by the back door, and as apex I cannot leave it unanswered. Bingham says the requirement attaches to resolution, not to s.7 validity: a genuine new regulation without a pointer has, "in s.23's own terms, an unresolved trace", so the floor never attached and nothing is narrowed. The argument contains a category slip, and naming it is the work of this judgment. Whether the trace is "unresolved" depends on what the trace IS for the regulation class. Bingham has already told us, correctly, that the trace is the route-class trace to the recorded Framework terminal. A genuine new regulation made under s.7 satisfies that trace; it resolves at the class level. Bingham cannot adopt the route-class reading of "trace" to establish the meaning and then silently switch to the per-instrument reading of "trace" to deny a genuine new regulation its floor. Held consistently, the route-class reading means a genuine new regulation resolves whether or not it carries a per-instrument note, and stripping its floor for want of that note is precisely the narrowing s.2 forbids. The "floor never attached" conclusion is true only on Move 2's premise, which is the contested point, not a given. So Bingham's mechanism, applied to new regulations, collapses into Move 2 and carries Move 2's narrowing. That is why I part from his disposition while adopting his diagnosis.

My Q2 holding, therefore: a route-class check (with the established-canon carve-out, and class-resolution for genuine new regulations) is faithful interpretation and non-narrowing, within this Council's power; a per-instrument provenance record as a condition of force is a narrowing of the entrenched floor reserved exclusively to the Sovereign. The Privy's divided Q2 is reconciled, not by choosing a side, but by cutting at the joint they all gestured at and none isolated.

I may not narrow that floor by machinery; if my faithful conclusion required it, I would have to reserve it to the Sovereign and say so. Second, the deterministic-clerk limit: the kernel may load, validate, resolve, route, and prove, but it may not judge merits, call a model, or create force by computation (ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.11; REG-KERNEL-001; INV-KERNEL-001). Third, the machinery-amends-nothing limit: no kernel act, agent act, or statutory instrument may amend a statute, a bench, a tier, or the assent rule (ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7, s.25; ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.14).

II. THE FACT THAT DECIDES THE CASE

The kernel today resolves a `standing_bounded_assent` claim on a single predicate, `founded`: true whenever the corpus contains any sovereign_assent_event or royal_assent anywhere (assent.rs line 163, 182). The established-at-HEAD carve-out, which protects an edit to a committed record from being narrowed, is wired only into the sovereign_assent arm (line 177), not the standing arm. When a record's assent resolves, lib.rs (lines 241 to 256) downgrades every non-constitutive blocking finding on it to route-for-correction, while the constitutive codes are never softened. So the residual is precise: a non-order record (a regulation) that merely types the two known words has any non-constitutive Fatal laundered to a Warning and commits. My fact-check confirms the corpus the cure must not break: thirty REG-* records carry the standing form, only six are individually named, and the rest are committed at HEAD but named by nothing. That asymmetry is the whole case, exactly as all three Privy judges found.

III. THE THREE MECHANISMS MUST BE TOLD APART, AND THAT IS THE KEY TO THE FRACTURE

The Privy split because its members were, at the decisive step, measuring three different mechanisms against the floor without naming them as three. I name them.

M1, the status quo: resolve the standing form on the bare `founded` predicate. All three Privy judges hold this is an under-implementation of s.23's "tracing" verb, of the same family PC-16 condemned for the sister clause. I agree, and I record it as common ground: an entrenched term cannot mean a real trace in its first limb (sovereign_assent) and "a foundation exists somewhere" in its second (standing_bounded_assent). Position B, that the permissive check is the faithful reading, is rejected.

M2, the route-class check: resolve the standing form if and only if (i) the record is established at HEAD (committed canon, which itself traces to the founding commencement lock under Bill 32's Sovereign assent), or (ii) for a fresh record, it declares a pointer that resolves against the recorded terminal for its class, for regulations the pinned framework_act_text_digest at COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002, for orders issuance by a constituted bench. A fresh record that resolves to neither has, in s.23's own words, "an unresolved trace," gets no shelter, and is routed for correction.

M3, the per-instrument force-gate: require each regulation to carry or be named by a separately-minted enactment-provenance event as a condition of force, so that a regulation validly made under s.7 but lacking that separate record loses its floor.

The fracture dissolves once these are kept apart. Atkin holds M3 narrows and is barred, and says M2 is faithful "but pointless." Bingham holds M2 is faithful and non-narrowing and builds it. Denning holds M2 is faithful and non-narrowing but reserves the build. The two Privy majorities (Atkin and Denning for the disposition; Bingham and Denning for the interpretation) were not contradicting each other on the merits. They were attacking different mechanisms. M2 is not M3.

IV. Q2, THE CONSTITUTIONAL CRUX: M2 IS INTERPRETATION; M3 IS NARROWING

I take Q2 first because it decides what Q1 may adopt.

Atkin's reading of s.7 is correct as far as it goes, and I adopt it: a regulation is constituted by being validly made under the standing power, with no fresh per-instrument Sovereign act, unlike the s.14 gate for primary law. The Sovereign assented once, to the Framework Act, and a class of subordinate records stands behind that one assent. The adjective "specific" in s.23 attaches to the terminal Sovereign assent the route ends in, the Framework assent recorded at COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002 and pinned by framework_act_text_digest, not to a separate per-instrument ceremony beneath it. From this Atkin draws the right conclusion about M3 and the wrong conclusion about M2.

The right conclusion: M3 narrows. To require a separately-minted provenance naming event as a condition of force is to invent a gating fact s.7 does not impose, and to strip a genuine new regulation's floor for the want of a third party's blessing the parent statute never demanded. That removes protection from a record genuinely made under the standing power, which is a narrowing of VJS-ACT 10 s.1 for a genuine record, reserved exclusively to the Sovereign. I adopt Atkin's reasoning on this point in full. M3 is barred to the bench and the clerk alike.

The wrong conclusion: that M2 is therefore "pointless," or that the only thing that closes the residual is the barred M3. This is where I part from Atkin and join Bingham and Denning, and I do so for reasons grounded in the enacted text, not in appetite to tidy.

M2 does not require a separate provenance ceremony. It requires the record to present a resolvable trace, by being committed canon at HEAD (which carries every existing regulation, the twenty-plus unnamed REG-* records included, because committed canon was brought into force by the commencement lock under the founding assent, a true trace and not a grandfather clause of convenience), or, for a fresh record, by declaring a pointer to the already-recorded Framework terminal. That declaration is within the author's own gift, exactly as PC-16 required a new Act to carry its own instrument_digest. It demands no new ceremony, no third party, and no fresh Sovereign act.

Is requiring that declaration of a new regulation narrowing, or is it interpretation? I hold it is interpretation, for three reasons rooted in the Sovereign's own words.

First, the text. s.23 itself enumerates "an unresolved trace" as a ground of rejection, standing shoulder to shoulder with absence and emptiness. That is the Sovereign's language, not the kernel's invention. A record that declares the standing form but presents nothing the deterministic clerk can resolve to its terminal is, in s.23's own express terms, an unresolved trace. The Sovereign legislated for exactly this case.

Second, the structure. s.23 is an affirmative allow-list that fails closed: a record "claims binding V2 runtime force only if it declares an assent_source resolving to ... or tracing to" specific Sovereign assent. The burden is on the record to declare a resolving or tracing source. Force is claimed by declaration, not presumed from off-record facts the clerk cannot see. The Realm chose this fail-closed form and rejected the fail-open deny-list shape ([2026] REALM-SC 10). A regulation that is "on the route in law" but presents no resolvable trace to the clerk has not met the burden s.23 places on it.

Third, the binding precedent on all fours. PC-16's ratio for sovereign_assent is that a declared assent resolving to nothing is an unresolved trace and earns no shelter, because s.2 confers no force on a record lacking a valid source, so keeping it at native severity voids nothing: a false signature is no signature. PC-16 said in terms that s.23's unresolved-trace language reaches both forms. To read standing_bounded_assent as satisfied by off-record valid-making while sovereign_assent demands a resolvable pointer would split one entrenched term into two inconsistent meanings. Constitutional hygiene forbids it.

It follows that keeping a non-resolving new regulation at native severity narrows nothing, because the s.1 floor reaches only records "within the meaning of s.23," and a regulation with an unresolved trace is, by s.23's own words, outside that meaning. There is nothing to void because no resolved assent was ever sheltered; a trace that points nowhere is, in law, no trace. The cure is route-for-correction: the author declares the pointer s.23 always demanded, and the floor attaches. That is the PC-16 move, faithfully transposed.

I therefore resolve the divided Q2 as follows. A route-class check (M2), built with the established-canon carve-out sheltering every existing in-force regulation and with route-for-correction (never void) for a non-resolving new one, is faithful interpretation of s.23, within this Council's interpretive power, and is non-narrowing. A per-instrument force-gate (M3) that strips a genuine new regulation's floor for want of a separately-minted provenance event would narrow VJS-ACT 10 s.1 and is reserved exclusively to the Sovereign. Both Privy majorities were right, about different mechanisms.

I. The absolutes I hold before me

PROPOSITION 2, the per-instrument force-gate. A regulation's force depends on a separately-committed per-instrument enactment-provenance record, and the ABSENCE of that record strips the floor from a regulation, including a genuine new one validly made under s.7.

But every existing regulation is established in committed canon, lodged at or after commencement, and committed canon is itself the sealed lawpack COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002 brought into force under the Framework and Bill 32 assents (the fresh lawpack lock sha256:8d075f7d... records exactly that sealing). Established-at-HEAD is therefore not a grandfather clause of convenience; it IS the recorded trace to the founding assent for the existing corpus. Extending it to the `standing_bounded_assent` arm resolves every existing regulation without narrowing one of them. A forged record is a fresh insertion with no committed history and fails it. So construed, Proposition 1 removes shelter from no genuine record: an existing regulation passes by established-at-HEAD; a genuine new one passes by pointing at the pinned Framework terminal, the same trivial discipline PC-16 imposed on a new Act; and a record that points nowhere has, in s.23's own terms, an unresolved trace, so by s.2 the floor never attached and there is nothing to void. That is interpretation, not amendment. It changes no word of VJS-ACT 10, s.23, or INV-ASSENT-SOURCE-001.

On PROPOSITION 2 I hold, with Atkin J., that a per-instrument force-gate WOULD narrow VJS-ACT 10 s.1 and is reserved exclusively to the Sovereign. A regulation is constituted by valid making under s.7, not by a fresh per-instrument record; s.7 demands parent authority, not a per-instrument ceremony, and certainly not a per-instrument provenance note. To make the ABSENCE of such a note strip the floor from a regulation genuinely made under the standing power is to invent a gating fact the parent statute does not impose, and to read "tracing to specific Sovereign assent" as if it said "tracing, per instrument, to a separately recorded enactment." That narrows the protection of a genuine record. It is constitutive change, reserved to a Sovereign-assented Act citing the floor by number. The bench and the clerk may not do it.

This is the disambiguation that settles the Privy's divided Q2. The route-class check (Proposition 1) does NOT require the per-instrument force-gate (Proposition 2). It resolves a new regulation on a class fact, the Framework-terminal pointer, never on the presence of a separately-committed enactment event. So the interpretive door Bingham and Denning held open is genuinely open, and the narrowing Atkin rightly feared lives only in Proposition 2, which I keep firmly shut. To the divided Q2 I answer: a route-class check, built with the established-canon carve-out and route-for-correction for new records, is within this Council's interpretive power and is non-narrowing; a per-instrument force-gate is not.

IV. The honest limit, which I will not let the order conceal

Atkin J. is right on one further point, and I adopt it because candour is part of the fidelity. Proposition 1 does NOT fully close the residual the submission targets. A determined author with write access can point a forged REG at the recorded Framework terminal in a well-formed, self-naming subordinate instrument, and it will resolve. The route-class check RAISES the forgery bar, from "type two known words" to "forge a well-formed, self-naming instrument that points at the pinned Framework terminal as a non-repudiable committed act," but it does not eliminate the residual. The only machinery that would close the remainder is the per-instrument force-gate, and that narrows a genuine record. So the residual that survives Proposition 1 is, for the regulation class, irreducible by lawful machinery. Its backstops are non-machine and already in place: the constitutive-validity codes (which void any forged ORDER independent of any trace, the high-value vector all three judges and I agree is already closed), the Sovereign's gate at s.14, and the continuing duty of reasonable skill and care (ACT-003 s.4 to s.8, breach self-fileable). This is the same candour PC-16 recorded for its self-satisfiable kernel edit, and the cure must not be represented as more than it is.

Here I side with Bingham J. on disposition over the reserve favoured by Atkin J. and Denning J., and I give my reasons for declining their reservation on this narrow point.

Denning J.'s reservation rests on two pillars: the floor's own ordering of errors (over-sheltering a forgery is the safer error, so prefer the permissive status quo), and the absence of a demonstrated need (the speclaw-3 discipline). Both pillars are weaker for Proposition 1 than for the per-instrument force-gate they were reasoned against. The error-ordering argument has force only where the tightening risks under-sheltering a GENUINE record. With the carve-out doing complete work, Proposition 1 under-shelters no existing genuine record (all resolve by established-at-HEAD) and no genuine new record that points at the Framework terminal (a trivial cure). The only records it leaves at native severity are forgeries, which were never entitled to anything, and a hypothetical new regulation whose author neglected the pointer, for which by s.23 and s.2 the floor never attached, so there is nothing to narrow. Once the narrowing risk is removed by the carve-out, the premise that powers "over-shelter is safer" is gone. As to demonstrated need, the speclaw-3 discipline guards against premature genericisation, building speculative abstraction ahead of a real fact-pattern. Proposition 1 is the opposite: it is the completion of a resolution mechanism the Realm already enacted and already built, applied to its sister clause, using a test (`established_at_head`) that already exists in the code. For this Council to declare the route-class reading the faithful one and then leave the kernel performing the very reading PC-16 condemned, on the bench's appetite to wait for a breach, would be incoherent and would itself fall below that standard. The meaning of an entrenched term is not contingent on a breach occurring.

I therefore reject Position B (the permissive trace is not the faithful reading; it is the conservative status quo, a different and lesser claim, and an under-implementation of an entrenched verb). I. The absolutes I hold in front of me

Four things bind me as hard limits and I test every step against them. First, the entrenched floor: ACT-ASSENTED-RECORD-PROTECTION (VJS-ACT 10) s.1 forbids any subordinate validation, gate, invariant, regulation, or kernel operation from voiding, excluding, or blocking a record that declares a valid assent_source within the meaning of s.23; s.2 confers no force on a record that lacks one and is amendable only by a Sovereign-assented constitutional Act citing it by number. II. Q2 first, because it is dispositive: interpretation or narrowing

I take the constitutional crux before the disposition, because whether the tightening is available at all decides what may be adopted.

The case turns on a distinction the Privy Council saw but did not cleanly separate, and I now draw it as the ratio. There are two different moves in play, not one.

The first move (the route-class check) is to resolve a regulation's `standing_bounded_assent` not on "some founding assent exists somewhere in the corpus" but on "this record is on the recorded standing route for its class, whose specific terminal Sovereign assent is recorded." For the regulation class the terminal is the one Framework Act assent, recorded at COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002 and pinned by framework_act_text_digest sha256:90e843c1dbdcdb2e2c7026d1064a457cf7ea1dbfe80526eff96b1f7e03850470. That terminal is shared by every regulation. A genuine regulation traces to it by its class; the maker names the route, it does not mint a fresh fact.

The second move (the per-instrument force-gate) is to require each regulation to carry, or be named by, its own per-instrument enactment-provenance event, the absence of which strips the floor.

These are constitutionally different, and the whole matter turns on keeping them apart.

I begin with what PC-16 actually did and why it was non-narrowing, because the analogy is the engine of the argument and it must be handled exactly. ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.14 makes a fresh, specific, per-instrument Sovereign Assent the sole constitutive gate of each primary or constitutional Act. The per-instrument assent event is the force-creating fact. Requiring a sovereign_assent record to resolve to that event therefore required proof of the very fact that gives the record force; it added no formality, and every genuine sovereign_assent in canon already carried its event and resolved cleanly. That is why PC-16 was pure interpretation.

A regulation is constituted differently, and Atkin J. is right about the difference, though wrong about its consequence. Under ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7 the Sovereign is not in the loop for each regulation: "Primary law requires Sovereign assent; subordinate law requires this parent authority." The force-creating fact for a regulation is valid making under the standing power, not a fresh per-instrument ceremony and not a per-instrument record. It follows, and I hold, that the per-instrument force-gate (the second move) WOULD narrow VJS-ACT 10 s.1. To make the absence of a bespoke per-instrument provenance event strip the floor from a regulation genuinely made under s.7 is to invent a new gating fact the parent statute does not impose and to read "tracing to specific Sovereign assent" as if it said "tracing, per instrument, to a separately recorded enactment ceremony." That is reserved exclusively to the Sovereign. On that narrow holding the whole Privy bench is in truth agreed, and I adopt it as common ground: Atkin states it directly, and Bingham and Denning each bar the force-gate as the line they will not cross.

But Atkin then conflates the second move with the first, and on that conflation I part from him. He reasons that the route-class check does not FULLY close the forgery residual (true, because a forged REG pointing at the terminal still resolves), that only per-instrument resolution closes it (true), and therefore that the route-class check should not be built at all. That proves too little. The fact that the route-class check does not fully shut the residual is not a reason to leave an entrenched verb under-implemented; it is a reason to build the move that honours the verb and is non-narrowing, and then to record candidly that the move which would fully close it is barred as narrowing.

And the route-class check IS non-narrowing, for a reason that is a fortiori from PC-16. The route-class check requires only what s.23 demands of every standing_bounded_assent record without exception: that its trace RESOLVE to a recorded terminal. It does not require a bespoke per-instrument fact; it accepts the shared, already-recorded Framework terminal, reached either by the record being established in committed canon or by the record declaring the route to that terminal. A genuine regulation carries no heavier burden than naming the one terminal that already exists. If PC-16's heavier requirement (point at a per-instrument event that had itself to exist) was non-narrowing interpretation, then pointing at a shared terminal that is already recorded is more plainly non-narrowing still.

I confront Atkin's sharpest form squarely: a regulation genuinely made under s.7 has force the moment it is made, with no per-instrument record required, so does requiring it to resolve to the recorded route strip the floor from a genuine record? No, and the answer is the same one PC-16 gave for the sister clause. A genuinely-assented Act also has force the moment the Sovereign assents, yet PC-16 held the deterministic clerk may still require the trace to RESOLVE, treating an unresolved trace as outside the floor, because the clerk acts on what resolves to it, not on what is true in the world but unrecorded to it. Section 23 itself lists "an unresolved trace" as a ground of rejection standing shoulder to shoulder with absence and emptiness, and section 2 confers no force on a record lacking a valid assent_source. A regulation that declares `standing_bounded_assent` but does not, to the clerk, trace to the recorded route has an unresolved trace; by s.23's own words it is outside the meaning of s.23; by s.2's own words the Act confers it no force; so keeping its native severity narrows nothing, because s.1 never reached it. There is nothing to void because there was never a resolved assent to shelter. A trace that points nowhere the clerk can follow is, to the clerk, no trace. That is the Bingham and Denning reasoning on Q2, and it is correct.

There is one more point I make load-bearing, because the ratio rests on it. The established-canon carve-out is not a grandfather clause of convenience; it is a true application of the rule. Every committed regulation at HEAD was brought into force by the commencement lock (COMMENCEMENT-V2-0001/0002), which itself traces to the Framework Sovereign assent and behind it Bill 32. So `established_at_head` IS, for the regulation class, a genuine recorded trace to the terminal. The carve-out does not suspend the resolution standard for existing regulations; it resolves them correctly under it. That is why extending it to the standing_bounded_assent branch narrows not one of the genuine records my fact-check enumerated.

I therefore decide Q2: requiring a regulation's `standing_bounded_assent` to resolve by route-class to the specific recorded Framework Sovereign assent is faithful INTERPRETATION of s.23, within this Council's interpretive power, and NON-narrowing, provided (1) the established-canon carve-out shelters every regulation committed at HEAD, (2) a non-resolving new regulation is surfaced and routed for correction, never voided or blocked, its underlying finding keeping native severity only because the floor never attached, and (3) the terminal anchor is the specific, recorded, digest-pinned Framework assent and the check stays pure clerk. The per-instrument force-gate is NARROWING and is reserved exclusively to the Sovereign. I adopt the Bingham and Denning interpretation majority on the route-class limb; I adopt Atkin and the common ground on the force-gate limb. The matter is not routed to the Sovereign, because the cure imposes only the route-class check, never the force-gate.

I. WHAT IS ACTUALLY IN DISPUTE

The kernel resolves a `standing_bounded_assent` claim on one predicate, `founded`, true whenever the corpus contains any `sovereign_assent_event` or `royal_assent` (assent.rs lines 179 to 183). The submission asks whether that should be tightened so a regulation must trace, by a recorded per-class route, to the Framework Act's s.7 power and on to the recorded Framework assent, on the model of [2026] VJS-PC 16's treatment of `sovereign_assent`. The stated prize is to close one narrow residual: the laundering of a non-constitutive Fatal onto a non-order record (a regulation) by typing the two allow-listed words.

I hold three things in front of me as absolutes throughout. First, the entrenched floor: ACT-ASSENTED-RECORD-PROTECTION s.1 routes a record with a valid assent_source for correction, never voiding or blocking it; s.2 confers no force on a record lacking one, and the Act is amendable only by a Sovereign-assented constitutional Act citing it by number. Second, the competence limits: the kernel is the deterministic clerk and judges no merits (REG-KERNEL-001), and machinery amends no statute, bench, tier, or assent rule (ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7, s.25; ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.14). Third, the discipline the Privy itself set at PC-16 D7 and which I make my own: if a faithful build cannot tighten without narrowing the protection of a genuinely-assented record, the builder must stop and route to the Sovereign. I may settle this matter either way with binding force, but I may not narrow the entrenched floor by machinery; if my faithful conclusion required that, I must reserve it to the Sovereign and say so.

II. Q2 FIRST, BECAUSE IT GOVERNS EVERYTHING (INTERPRETATION vs NARROWING)

I take the constitutional crux first, because whether a tightening is even available decides what Q1 may adopt, and because the Privy's three-way split is, at bottom, a split on this single question that the bench could not resolve because it never isolated the fact that decides it. I now isolate it.

PC-16's resolution of `sovereign_assent` was clean interpretation for a reason rooted in s.14. Each primary or constitutional Act is constituted by a fresh, specific, per-instrument Sovereign Assent against that Act's own digest. Every genuine `sovereign_assent` therefore leaves a per-instrument witness, and, crucially, each Act's terminal is a DIFFERENT event. Because the terminal is per-instrument and variable, a coarse "some foundation exists" check genuinely fails to resolve WHICH event is meant; the trace is, in s.23's own words, "unresolved." Requiring the per-instrument pointer there added no formality. It enforced the very constitutive act s.14 already demands, and it stripped protection from nothing real, because every genuine record carries the matching digest. That is why PC-16 was faithful interpretation.

A regulation is constituted differently, and the difference is the whole case. ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7 provides that the Standing Committee may make subordinate instruments under the Act, and that "Primary law requires Sovereign assent; subordinate law requires this parent authority"; its kernel_effect declares `si_power: standing_bounded` and `subordinate_requires_parent_authority`. The Sovereign is, by deliberate design, NOT in the loop for each regulation. The Sovereign assented ONCE, to the Framework Act and its machinery power. A regulation acquires force by being validly made under that parent authority, with no fresh per-instrument Sovereign act. That is what the word "standing" in `standing_bounded_assent` denotes: one specific Sovereign assent standing behind a whole CLASS.

The consequence is decisive, and it is the ratio of this opinion. For the regulation class the route terminal is not per-instrument and variable; it is CLASS-WIDE, FIXED, and RECORDED, namely the one Framework assent at COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002 (framework_act_text_digest sha256:90e843c1...). A regulation on the s.7 route therefore has a trace that is RESOLVED BY ITS CLASS MEMBERSHIP: the kernel already knows, deterministically, the single terminal the entire class points at. Such a record does not have an "unresolved trace" within s.23 merely because it lacks a per-instrument pointer to a terminal that is already determinate from the class. This is the precise inverse of `sovereign_assent`, where the terminal is unknowable without the pointer. The asymmetry between a variable per-instrument terminal and a single recorded class terminal is the fact the Privy never isolated, and it disposes of Q2.

From that asymmetry two holdings follow, and they map onto the two different things the word "tighten" was concealing.

(1) A route-CLASS check, one that resolves a regulation's `standing_bounded_assent` on its being a record on the recognised s.7 route whose single recorded terminal (the Framework assent) exists, is faithful interpretation of s.23 and within this Council's interpretive power, and it narrows no genuine record. The present `founded` predicate is a coarse implementation of exactly this: it confirms that the Realm's Sovereign foundation, of which the Framework assent is a part, exists. For a regulation on the s.7 route that is a true, if blunt, confirmation that the class terminal is recorded. It is therefore NOT the per-instrument-variable defect PC-16 condemned, and Position B's claim that it is the FAITHFUL reading and Position A's claim that it is the IDENTICAL DEFECT are both wrong in the same way: the first understates and the second overstates. The truth is that `founded` is coarse but faithful for a class whose terminal is singular and recorded.

(2) A per-instrument FORCE-GATE, one that strips the floor or holds a Fatal at Fatal for a regulation that lacks its OWN committed enactment-provenance record, is NOT interpretation. It invents a gating fact s.7 does not impose. Section 7 makes a regulation's force turn on valid making under the parent authority, not on a per-instrument paper trail; there is no per-instrument constitutive act for such a record to witness. To make the absence of that paper strip the floor of a regulation genuinely made under s.7 is to narrow ACT-ASSENTED-RECORD-PROTECTION s.1 for a genuine record. That is constitutive change reserved exclusively to the Sovereign (Framework s.25; VJS-ACT 10 s.2). On this Atkin J. is right, and his conclusion is the entrenched-floor absolute I am forbidden to cross by machinery.

So I resolve the Privy's divided Q2 in full: both Privy majorities were half-right about DIFFERENT mechanisms. The route-class check (Bingham J. and Denning J.) is interpretation; the per-instrument force-gate (Atkin J.) is narrowing. They appeared to disagree because each used "tighten" for a different machine, and neither named the singular-versus-variable terminal that separates the two. I have now named it. A route-class check is within the Council's interpretive power; a per-instrument force-gate is not, and is reserved to a Sovereign-assented Act.

III. THE FINDING THAT DISPOSES OF Q1: THE RESIDUAL IS IRREDUCIBLE BY LAWFUL MACHINERY

Having separated the two machines, I now press the question the submission actually asks: which of them CLOSES the stated residual, the laundering of a non-constitutive Fatal onto a forged non-order regulation?

The answer, which I verified against the kernel's own logic, is that the route-class check does NOT close it. A forger with write access who stages a fresh REG-* need only type `standing_bounded_assent` and point at the single, publicly recorded Framework terminal, the very terminal the genuine class shares. The route-class check cannot tell that forger apart from a genuine author, because the only fact that distinguishes them, whether the instrument was in truth validly MADE by the Standing Committee under s.7, is a merits question the clerk may not judge (REG-KERNEL-001). The route-class check thus raises the forgery bar from "type two words" to "type two words and one public digest," a marginal increase that leaves the residual open. The ONLY machine that genuinely closes the residual is the per-instrument force-gate, which demands a separately committed enactment record the forger has not also planted. And that machine is precisely the one Part II holds narrows a genuine new regulation and is barred to the bench.

This is the hinge of the whole matter and I state it as a binding holding: for the regulation class, the laundering residual cannot be closed by lawful machinery. The non-narrowing machine does not close it; the machine that would close it narrows a genuine record and is reserved to the Sovereign. Atkin J. reached this and I adopt it. It follows that no argument from "we must build something to close the residual" can succeed, because the residual is, for this class, irreducible by the clerk. The submission's prize is unreachable by the route it proposes.

IV. Q1: WHY I RESERVE THE REGULATION BUILD AND DECLINE EVEN THE NON-NARROWING REFINEMENT

That still leaves the narrower question Bingham J. pressed hardest: even granting that the route-class refinement closes nothing, should the kernel build it anyway, to honour the entrenched "tracing" verb the `founded` predicate under-implements, exactly as PC-16 honoured the sister clause? I have weighed his opinion as the strongest case for building, and I decline it, on three grounds that together are decisive.

First, Bingham J.'s argument rests on the premise that `founded` is the "identical defect" PC-16 condemned, so that leaving it falls below the standard of care. Part II shows that premise is false. The two limbs do not sit on the same constitutional ground: the `sovereign_assent` terminal is per-instrument and variable, where a coarse check truly fails to resolve; the regulation terminal is singular and recorded, where the coarse check coarsely-but-truly resolves by class. The "identical defect" never existed. The duty of care is therefore not engaged by leaving a faithful, if blunt, implementation in place, and I find no breach (Part V).

Second, the floor is not neutral between the two ways the kernel can err, and Denning J. is right about the ordering. The permissive check errs only by OVER-sheltering: at worst it downgrades a non-constitutive Fatal on a FORGED regulation, a fail-open on a surface the submission concedes is tiny, and one that never touches a genuine record. A tightening errs, if it errs, by UNDER-sheltering, that is, by narrowing a genuine record's floor. ACT-ASSENTED-RECORD-PROTECTION s.1 is an absolute against the second error and merely tolerant of the routed-for-correction defects that flow from the first. The floor's own architecture ranks narrowing a genuine record as the graver wrong. When in doubt, the floor-faithful direction of error is to over-shelter, which is exactly what the conservative status quo does. Building a refinement that changes almost nothing in behaviour (it would still resolve every regulation on the route, because the class terminal always exists) while carrying a nonzero risk that a later builder over-reaches the carve-out and strips a genuine record is to court the graver error to cure the lesser one.

Third, there is no demonstrated need, and the Realm's own discipline (the speclaw-3 test, and REG-KERNEL-001's "only smart enforcement point") counsels against genericising machinery in advance of one. No recorded regulation-laundering breach exists; the high-value order and apex vector is already closed at the constitutive rung; the residual is a non-constitutive Fatal on a non-order, a class whose Fatals are usually advisory Warnings already (S5_INERT_KERNEL_EFFECT is a Warning). PC-16 reserved this very tightening deliberately and lawfully, not by oversight. I do not disturb a reasoned reservation to tidy a residual that, on the verified facts, no one has exploited and that the clerk cannot fully close in any event.

I therefore dispose of Q1 as follows. For ORDERS: the standing_bounded_assent route is issuance by a constituted bench under ACT-002, and the forgery vector is already closed independently by the constitutive-validity codes (BENCH_REQUIRED, TIER_NOT_CONSTITUTED, BENCH_SIZE_MISMATCH, BENCH_SILENT_SEAT, BENCH_OPINION_MISSING, CITATION_COLLISION, APEX_RECORD_IN_SUBSCRIBING_JURISDICTION), which no assent claim of either form softens and which the end-to-end proofs already hold green. I affirm that closure and make it explicit; I add no order-specific resolution gate, which would be redundant and would import the same narrowing risk into the one class already perfectly closed. For REGULATIONS: the permissive `founded` trace stands as a faithful, if coarse, implementation of the class-route trace; I decline the per-instrument force-gate as barred narrowing; and I decline even the non-narrowing route-class refinement now, for the three grounds above, reserving it on the interpretive ratio and the binding non-narrowing conditions recorded below.
