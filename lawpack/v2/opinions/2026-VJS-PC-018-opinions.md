In the Reference re Standing-Route Resolution (SUBMISSION-2026-06-25-standing-route-resolution, case-file digest sha256:4344c70a40bde0080ca53e627c7bcd78a61caeae95b7fd6a49b47625e8a0b58c)

I

The matter, shorn of its framing, is a single question with a hard edge. The kernel today resolves a `standing_bounded_assent` claim permissively: `assent.rs::assent_resolves` confirms only that the Realm is Sovereign-founded (`corpus.contains("sovereign_assent_event") || corpus.contains("royal_assent")`). The submission asks whether the kernel should tighten that to "the record traces to a recorded per-class standing route", so that a regulation must carry or point at an enactment-provenance record placing it under ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7, on the model of [2026] VJS-PC 16's treatment of `sovereign_assent`. The stated prize is to close a narrow residual: laundering a non-constitutive Fatal on a non-order regulation by merely typing the two known words.

I keep three things in front of me throughout, as absolutes. First, the entrenched floor: ACT-ASSENTED-RECORD-PROTECTION s.1 says a record with a valid assent_source is routed for correction, never voided or blocked; s.2 says the Act confers no force on a record that lacks one, and is amendable only by a Sovereign-assented constitutional Act citing it by number. Second, the non-narrowing absolute that PC-16 set at D2/D7: the resolution gate may fail a record closed only on the ground that its assent does not resolve, and if a faithful build cannot tighten without narrowing the protection of a genuinely-assented record, the engineer must stop and route to the Sovereign. Third, the competence limits: the kernel is the deterministic clerk and may not judge merits (REG-KERNEL-001), and machinery may not amend a statute, bench, tier, or assent rule (ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7; ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.14).

II - Q2 first, because it is dispositive (INTERPRETATION vs NARROWING)

I take Q2 before Q1, because whether the tightening is even available decides what Q1 may adopt.

PC-16's move for `sovereign_assent` was faithful interpretation for a reason that does not travel to regulations. ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.14 makes a fresh, specific, datable, digest-pinnable ceremonial Sovereign Assent the sole constitutive gate for each primary or constitutional Act. A genuine assent of that kind always leaves a witness: the Sovereign said "I assent" against a digest, and the provenance event records it. Requiring the record therefore added no formality; it required proof of the constitutive act the statute already demands. That is why PC-16 could say, truthfully, that it "removes protection from no genuinely-assented record" - every real `sovereign_assent` in canon carries an instrument_digest, resolves cleanly, and keeps its floor. A false signature is no signature.

A regulation is constituted differently, and the difference is the whole case. I have read ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7. Its words are: "The Standing Committee may make statutory instruments and subordinate regulations under this Act ... Primary law requires Sovereign assent; subordinate law requires this parent authority." Its kernel_effect declares `si_power: standing_bounded` and `subordinate_requires_parent_authority: true`. The Sovereign is, by design, NOT in the loop for each regulation. The Sovereign assented ONCE, to the Framework Act and its machinery power, and subordinate regulations acquire force by being validly made under that parent authority, without any fresh per-instrument Sovereign act. That is precisely what the word "standing" in `standing_bounded_assent` denotes: one specific Sovereign assent standing behind a CLASS of subordinate records.

Read s.23 against that. A valid `standing_bounded_assent` is "a standing-bounded route tracing to specific Sovereign assent". The adjective "specific" attaches to the Sovereign assent the route terminates in, not to a per-instrument paper trail beneath it. A regulation made under s.7 traces to a specific Sovereign assent: the Framework Act assent, recorded at `COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002` and pinned by `framework_act_text_digest` sha256:90e843c1.... That assent is specific, identifiable, and digest-pinned. The trace exists in law for every regulation genuinely made under the parent power, whether or not that particular regulation also carries its own provenance note.

It follows that requiring a per-instrument enactment-provenance record as a CONDITION OF FORCE is not the same kind of move PC-16 made. For `sovereign_assent` the record merely witnesses a constitutive act the statute independently demands. For a regulation there is no per-instrument constitutive act to witness: s.7 demands parent authority, not a fresh enactment ceremony, and certainly not a fresh provenance record. To make the absence of such a record strip the floor is to invent a new gating fact the parent statute does not impose, and to read "tracing to specific Sovereign assent" as if it said "tracing, per instrument, to a separately recorded enactment". That is not giving an entrenched verb its meaning; it is adding a formality to it.

And the cost lands exactly where D7 warned it would. The facts (F2), which I verified, are that many genuine REG-* records are not individually named by any provenance event. The established-at-HEAD carve-out rescues those EXISTING records - and rightly, because a committed regulation is part of the lawpack the commencement lock brought into force, so established-at-HEAD is itself a true trace to the Framework assent, not a grandfather-clause of convenience. But Position A does not stop at existing records: it requires enactment provenance for NEW regulations. A future regulation genuinely made by the Standing Committee under s.7, but whose author does not also mint a provenance note, would fail to resolve and lose its floor. That record IS genuinely assented in the only sense s.7 requires - it was made under the parent authority - and stripping its protection because it lacks paper the parent statute never required is a narrowing of VJS-ACT 10 s.1 for a genuine record. Under the PC-16 D2/D7 absolute, that crosses from interpretation into constitutive change. It is reserved to the Sovereign; the bench and the clerk may not do it.

I have asked myself the steelman: is there a non-narrowing way to close the regulation residual? There is a faithful intermediate refinement - call it the recognised-route check - under which `standing_bounded_assent` resolves not on "some founding assent exists anywhere" but on "this record is on a recognised standing route whose specific terminal assent is recorded" (an order from a constituted bench; a REG declaring the s.7 power, terminating at the recorded Framework assent). That reading has genuine textual support, because the present `founded` check confirms only a generic fact and arguably reads the "tracing" verb as thin as the old code read "resolving". But the recognised-route check, even if built, does NOT close the residual the submission targets: a forged or defective REG on the s.7 route would still resolve and could still launder a non-constitutive Fatal. The ONLY thing that closes the regulation-laundering residual is per-instrument resolution - and per-instrument resolution is the very thing that narrows a genuine unrecorded regulation. So I say plainly, as D7 requires: the build cannot tighten the regulation limb enough to close the residual without narrowing a genuine record. The residual is, for the regulation class, irreducible by lawful machinery.

III - Q1 (ADOPT / TIGHTEN)

On Q1, then, I would not adopt the tightening as a force-gate for regulations, and I do not need to for orders.

The high-value vector is already closed, and closed at the right place. The constitutive codes (`BENCH_REQUIRED`, `TIER_NOT_CONSTITUTED`, `BENCH_SIZE_MISMATCH`, `BENCH_SILENT_SEAT`, `BENCH_OPINION_MISSING`, `CITATION_COLLISION`, `APEX_RECORD_IN_SUBSCRIBING_JURISDICTION`) are never softened by any assent claim, of either form. A bench-less or never-convened order, or an apex record in a subscribing jurisdiction, is void on a ground that goes to whether it IS an order at all, independent of how permissively the trace resolves. The proof tests already exist end-to-end (`forged_standing_bounded_order_is_still_blocked`, `constitutive_bench_defect_not_downgraded_even_for_an_established_assented_order`). The orders limb therefore needs no new resolution gate; adding an order-specific resolution check would be redundant and, worse, would import the same paper-trail narrowing risk into the one class that is already perfectly closed. I decline to touch it.

The regulation limb I have already disposed of in Q2: as a force-gate it is barred. That leaves the permissive trace standing for regulations. I am candid that the present `founded` check is a partial under-implementation of the "tracing" verb, of the same family PC-16 condemned for `sovereign_assent`. But for the regulation class the verb cannot be fully honoured by machinery without either narrowing genuine records (per-instrument resolution) or a Sovereign amendment that changes the standard. PC-16 deliberately and lawfully left this permissive and reserved the tightening; it was a reasoned reservation, not an oversight, and I do not disturb it.

IV - Q3 (MECHANISM + MIGRATION), and the Position I adopt

I adopt POSITION C, refined. Position A is rejected: its regulation limb narrows VJS-ACT 10 s.1 for a genuine future regulation and is reserved to the Sovereign, not interpretation within this bench's power. Position B is right in its central caution - any change to the entrenched term's resolution that could narrow the floor is reserved to a Sovereign-assented Act - and I adopt that caution for the per-instrument limb; but B is incomplete, because the orders vector IS closed (by the constitutive codes, not by the permissive trace) and that should be stated, and because B risks reading the residual as simply "accepted forever" rather than naming precisely why it is irreducible by machinery. Position C captures the right structure: affirm what is already closed, record the regulation route as reserved, and build nothing now absent demonstrated need (the speclaw-3 discipline). I strengthen C in one respect that the framing understates: the regulation per-instrument force-gate is not merely "reserved until a breach is shown" - as a FORCE-GATE it is barred as narrowing, so even a future regulation-laundering breach does not authorise building it; the lawful cures are a Sovereign amendment or a non-narrowing advisory, never a machinery-built force strip.

What is recorded now: the routes are already set out in `docs/standing-routes-analysis.md` (orders to a constituted bench; regulations to the s.7 power terminating at the Framework assent; founding corpus to the commencement records), and that note correctly identifies the residual and the interim backstop (constitutive codes plus the duty of care, ACT-003 s.4-s.8). This judgment adds the holding that closes the question: per-instrument enactment provenance is a permitted GOOD PRACTICE and audit attachment for new regulations, but the kernel may never make its ABSENCE strip a record's floor.

What triggers a future build: not a breach, but a Principal/Sovereign choice between two lawful routes - a Sovereign-assented amendment to the resolution standard, or a non-narrowing advisory that surfaces (never blocks) the lack of provenance. The speclaw-3 test independently confirms reservation: there is no demonstrated regulation-laundering breach, the residual surface is tiny (regulation Fatals are usually advisory Warnings already), and the dangerous class is closed - so there is no demonstrated need to build genericisation now even setting the narrowing bar aside.

In the matter of the standing-route resolution of standing_bounded_assent (SUBMISSION-2026-06-25-standing-route-resolution), the residual reserved by [2026] VJS-PC 16 at D4/D7.

I sit on the constituted odd bench of three. I have read the symmetric case file in full, verified the cited law and the cited kernel code with my own hand, and decided on that record alone. I carry no preference into this matter and have had no sight of the advocate-engineer's preference. I write as a faithful judge of an entrenched term, conscious that the easy instinct, to close every residual, is precisely the instinct an entrenched floor exists to discipline.

THE GROUND I STAND ON

Three things are settled before me and I do not reopen them. First, by [2026] VJS-PC 16, "valid assent_source" within ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.23, as imported by VJS-ACT 10 s.1, is not bare membership of the allow-list; the declared assent must RESOLVE. Section 23 names "an unresolved trace" as a ground of rejection standing shoulder to shoulder with absence and emptiness. Second, the high-value vector, a bench-less or never-convened order, apex or otherwise, is closed independently of any assent claim by the constitutive-validity codes (assent.rs CONSTITUTIVE_CODES; the e2e proofs forged_fresh_apex_order_stays_fatal_through_the_pipeline, forged_standing_bounded_order_is_still_blocked, constitutive_bench_defect_not_downgraded_even_for_an_established_assented_order). No assent of either form softens those findings. Third, the floor is entrenched: VJS-ACT 10 s.2 confers no force on a record lacking a valid assent_source, and it does not enlarge what the Sovereign assented to; equally, a record that HAS a valid (resolving) assent_source may never be voided or blocked, only routed for correction.

I also fix the operative fact the kernel presents today. In assent.rs the standing_bounded_assent branch resolves on a single predicate, `founded`, which is true whenever the corpus contains any sovereign_assent_event or royal_assent anywhere. The established-at-HEAD carve-out, which protects an edit to a commenced record from being narrowed, is wired only into the sovereign_assent branch (line 172), not the standing_bounded_assent branch (lines 174 to 178). That asymmetry is the whole of this case.

And I fix the constitutional source of a regulation's force. Framework Act s.7 provides that a statutory instrument "so made is subordinate law" and that "subordinate law requires this parent authority", the parent being the Sovereign-assented Framework Act itself (kernel_effect: si_power: standing_bounded; subordinate_requires_parent_authority). A regulation's force therefore traces, by its class route, to the specific Sovereign assent recorded at COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002 (framework_act_text_digest) and behind it Bill 32. That terminal is recorded and digest-pinned. What is NOT recorded, for most existing regulations, is the per-instrument link from the regulation to that terminal.

Q1, ADOPT OR TIGHTEN. Disposition: TIGHTEN. The permissive trace does not suffice.

The predicate "a sovereign_assent_event exists somewhere in the corpus" is, for the standing_bounded_assent limb, the identical defect PC-16 condemned for the sovereign_assent limb: it confirms that the Realm has a foundation, not that THIS record traces to it. "Tracing to specific Sovereign assent" is a transitive verb with an object; a check that is satisfied by the mere existence of a foundation reads the object out of the section exactly as bare membership read "resolving to" out of it. That the dangerous vector is already closed by the constitutive codes does not cure the under-implementation of the entrenched verb for the records the constitutive codes do not reach, namely a non-constitutive Fatal on a non-order record. An entrenched term cannot mean a real trace in its first limb and "a foundation exists" in its second. To that extent the kernel must resolve standing_bounded_assent through the RECORDED per-class route, not the bare `founded` predicate: for orders, issuance by a constituted bench under ACT-002 (already enforced by the constitutive codes); for regulations, the recorded Framework s.7 route to the Framework Act assent.

Q2, INTERPRETATION OR NARROWING. Disposition: faithful INTERPRETATION, within this Council's power, but ONLY because two absolutes hold; I engage PC-16 D7 squarely and find the build can tighten without narrowing a genuine record.

This is the hardest question and I have reasoned it hardest. The tempting answer, "it is the same move PC-16 made, therefore it is interpretation," is too quick, because the two limbs sit on opposite empirical ground. When PC-16 required sovereign_assent to resolve, every genuine sovereign_assent record in canon already carried or matched a pinned digest, so resolution stripped protection from nothing real; the move was non-narrowing on its face. Here the opposite is true on the existing corpus: most genuine regulations (REG-001 through 008, REG-KERNEL-001, REG-LAWMAKING-001, REG-SELF-CONVENE-001, and others) are NOT named by any per-instrument provenance event. A rule of "resolve only via a recorded per-class route record", applied flatly, would on its face strip the floor from a large body of genuinely-made subordinate law. That is the PC-16 D2/D7 line, and a flat rule would cross it.

The line is not crossed, and the move stays interpretation, because of two things that must both be true and which I make conditions of the holding.

First, the established-canon carve-out is load-bearing and absolute. Every existing regulation is established in committed canon; each was lodged at or after commencement, and committed canon itself traces to the founding Sovereign assent that commenced the lawpack. The established-at-HEAD test (assent.rs::established_at_head) IS, for the existing corpus, the recorded trace to the founding commencement. Extending that carve-out to the standing_bounded_assent branch resolves every existing regulation's trace without narrowing one of them. This is not a concession to convenience; it is the same reasoning PC-16 used to keep an edit to a commenced record at full height, transposed to the sister clause. A forged record is a fresh insertion with no committed history and fails this test; a genuine existing regulation passes it. The residual the submission identifies, a forged non-order record typing standing_bounded_assent, is a fresh insertion and is therefore closed by the carve-out itself.

Second, the requirement must attach to the kernel's RESOLUTION (which records the floor shelters), never as a new substantive condition on s.7 validity. Section 7 makes a regulation's force turn on its being "made under this Act"; the provenance record is evidence that resolves the trace, not a constitutive element of the making. A genuine new regulation that genuinely has s.7 force but does not point at its terminal has, in s.23's own terms, an "unresolved trace", and s.23 already makes that a ground of non-shelter. Keeping such a record at native severity narrows nothing, because the floor (s.1) reaches only records "within the meaning of s.23", and an unresolved trace is, by s.23, outside that meaning. There is nothing to void because there was never a resolved assent to shelter; a trace that points nowhere is, in law, no trace. The cure for the genuine author is trivial and identical in shape to the cure PC-16 imposed on a new Act: point at the pinned terminal. So the tightening removes shelter only from records that never traced, and that is interpretation, not amendment, changing no word of VJS-ACT 10, s.23, or INV-ASSENT-SOURCE-001.

I record candidly the irreducible remainder, as PC-16 did for its own residuals, because honesty about the limit is part of the fidelity. The deterministic clerk can confirm a new regulation's FORM (a subordinate instrument) and its TERMINAL (the pinned Framework digest); it cannot confirm the legislative WILL that it was in truth made by the Standing Committee under s.7. A determined author with write access could point a forged REG-* at the pinned Framework terminal. The tightening therefore raises the forgery bar from "type two known words" to "point at the recorded Framework terminal in a well-formed subordinate instrument that names itself", and makes a forged regulation a non-repudiable, self-naming act, but it does not, and cannot, fully bind that hand. The ultimate backstops there are non-machine: the Sovereign's gate at s.14 and the continuing duty of reasonable skill and care (ACT-003 s.4 to s.8, breach self-fileable). This is the same candour PC-16 recorded for the self-satisfiable kernel edit, and the cure must not be represented as more than it is.

Because both absolutes hold on the verified facts, the build can tighten without narrowing a genuine record. But the D7 duty is live and I direct it expressly below: if, in building, Lexby finds any genuine record, an existing regulation not caught by the carve-out, or a genuine new regulation with real s.7 force that cannot be made to resolve deterministically, that would lose the floor it is entitled to, that is the moment interpretation has crossed into constitutive change. Lexby must then stop, ship nothing, self-file, and route to the Sovereign; only a Sovereign-assented Act citing VJS-ACT 10 by number could make that change.

Q3, MECHANISM AND MIGRATION. Disposition: ADOPT, build now, as interpretation. The mechanism is symmetric with PC-16's and the migration narrows nothing.

Mechanism. Replace the bare `founded` predicate on the standing_bounded_assent branch with a recorded-route resolution, deterministic, model-free, network-free, clock-independent (INV-KERNEL-001, REG-KERNEL-001). For ORDERS the recorded route is a constituted bench, and that is already enforced by the constitutive codes; no new substantive code is required, only that this be made explicit and kept proven. For REGULATIONS the record resolves if (a) it is established at HEAD (the existing corpus and any committed regulation, tracing to commencement), OR (b) it declares a pointer that resolves against the recorded Framework s.7 terminal (the pinned framework_act_text_digest at COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002, or a per-instrument enactment-provenance record that itself is resolved, committed canon and not a co-staged forgery). A standing_bounded_assent record that is neither established nor points at the recorded route has an unresolved trace and keeps its native severity.

Migration. The established-canon carve-out IS the migration for every existing regulation, at zero narrowing and near-zero cost, because the test already exists in code and need only be added to the standing_bounded_assent branch. Enactment provenance is REQUIRED only of NEW regulations going forward, exactly as PC-16 required a new Act to carry its instrument_digest; the friction is the same trivial friction, and it falls only on records that have not yet traced. No existing genuine regulation is touched.

POSITION ADOPTED: A, TIGHTEN AS INTERPRETATION, with the orders limb recognised as already closed.

I adopt Position A by name. Requiring a standing_bounded_assent record to trace to a recorded per-class route is faithful interpretation of s.23, of the same character as PC-16's resolution of the sister clause, and within this Council's interpretive power; built with the established-canon carve-out it narrows no genuine record. I reject Position B: the permissive trace is not the irreducible remainder PC-16 accepted but an under-implementation of the entrenched verb, and constitutional hygiene forbids letting an entrenched term mean a real trace in one limb and "a foundation exists" in the other; the constitutive codes close the dangerous vector but do not license reading the trace out of the section for the records they do not reach. I reject Position C's reservation limb: I take up its true observation, that the orders route is already closed by the constitutive codes, but I decline to RESERVE the regulation build for a demonstrated breach. The meaning of an entrenched term is not contingent on a breach occurring; PC-16 settled the sister clause without waiting for one. Nor does the speclaw-3 demonstrated-need test counsel reservation, because this is not the premature genericisation that test guards against; it is the completion of the very resolution mechanism already built, applied to its sister clause, which is the opposite of speculative abstraction. The build is cheap, symmetric, and non-narrowing, and to leave a known under-implementation of an entrenched term in place after condemning the identical defect next door would itself fall below the standard of care.

I. The question, and the trap inside it

The Realm asks whether the kernel should tighten the resolution of `standing_bounded_assent` from the present "the realm is Sovereign-founded" to "the record traces to a recorded per-class standing route," so as to close the narrow residual by which a non-constitutive Fatal could be laundered onto a non-order record (a regulation) by typing the entrenched words. I begin by naming, plainly, what is and is not at stake, because the submission's own framing of Position A blends two moves that the entrenched floor treats very differently.

The dangerous vector is already shut. A bench-less, never-convened order, or an apex order in a subscribing jurisdiction, is barred by the constitutive-validity codes (assent.rs, CONSTITUTIVE_CODES), which PC-16 held are never softened by any assent claim of either form. I have read the codes and the e2e proofs cited in docs/standing-routes-analysis.md; the order class is closed independently of how permissively `standing_bounded_assent` resolves. What remains is small and conceded small: a non-constitutive Fatal on a regulation, and regulation Fatals are in practice usually advisory Warnings already.

II. The fidelity point that cuts against doing nothing

I do not accept that the present permissive check is a faithful reading of s.23. Section 23 admits `standing_bounded_assent` only as "a standing-bounded route tracing to specific Sovereign assent," and stands "an unresolved trace" shoulder to shoulder with absence and emptiness as a ground of rejection. The kernel's `founded` test (corpus contains `sovereign_assent_event` or `royal_assent`) does not follow the record's trace at all; it confirms only that the Realm possesses a foundation somewhere. That is the standing-bounded analogue of the precise defect PC-16 condemned for `sovereign_assent`: an existence check standing in for a resolution. So I reject Position B insofar as it asserts the permissive check is the faithful reading. It is not; it is under-faithful, and the Realm may cure it by interpretation. To that extent the interpretive door Position A pushes on is correctly hung.

III. Why the regulation route is not the sovereign_assent route, and where the line really runs (Q2)

But the move that closes the residual is not the move PC-16 made, and the difference is decisive. For `sovereign_assent`, resolution means pointing at a specific Sovereign-assent EVENT, and a genuinely assented Act always has one (the Sovereign actually assented; the event is recorded and digest-pinned). Genuine records therefore always resolved, and only forgeries lost anything; that is why PC-16 was pure interpretation.

A regulation is different in kind. A regulation under Framework s.7 is subordinate law; it receives no Sovereign assent of its own (s.7: "subordinate law requires this parent authority," not Sovereign assent). There is exactly one terminal Sovereign event on its route, and it is shared: the Framework assent of 2026-06-09 (COMMENCEMENT-V2-0002, framework_act_text_digest sha256:90e843c1...). The fact that distinguishes a genuine regulation from a forged one is therefore NOT a per-instrument Sovereign event (there is none) but the fact of valid subordinate making by the Standing Committee. Whether an instrument was validly made under s.7, and within its bounds, is a merits question the kernel may not judge (REG-KERNEL-001, the deterministic clerk). The only thing the clerk CAN check is whether a recorded enactment event exists for the instrument. So for regulations the proposed resolution does not merely "follow the trace to the Sovereign event"; it requires a recorded enactment record to stand in as proof of valid making. That proxy sits closer to the merits boundary and closer to the narrowing line than PC-16's clean digest-match ever did.

This is the heart of the matter, and I hold as follows. Requiring a `standing_bounded_assent` regulation to trace, BY ITS ROUTE-CLASS, to the specific recorded Framework Sovereign assent is faithful interpretation of s.23 and within this Council's power, and it does not narrow VJS-ACT 10 s.1, but ONLY if three absolutes hold together:

(1) Every regulation already committed in canon at HEAD is sheltered by the established-canon carve-out (the `established_at_head` pattern PC-16 already uses), so that not one existing in-force regulation loses its floor. My own fact-check makes this non-negotiable: a substantial set of in-force regulations, including the kernel's own REG-KERNEL-001, carry no per-instrument provenance event. A tightening that forgot the carve-out would strip the floor from those genuine records. That is not a hypothetical; it is the demonstrated hazard of building hastily.

(2) A new regulation whose trace does not resolve is ROUTED to record its enactment-provenance trace (correct the assent claim), never voided or erased. By s.23 its trace is unresolved at the moment of bare insertion, so the floor never attached (s.2: the Act "confers no force on any record that lacks a valid assent_source... it does not enlarge it"); the underlying finding keeps its native severity for that reason alone, and the cure is to add the one record that perfects the trace, exactly as COMMENCEMENT named the founding six in the same breath as lodging them. So construed, no genuine new regulation is narrowed: it is given the chance to perfect the very trace s.23 always demanded.

(3) The terminal anchor is a specific, recorded, digest-pinned Sovereign assent (the Framework assent for the s.7 route), and the check stays pure clerk: recorded-event lookup, digest compare, and the existing EntityScope/date coverage, with no judgment of whether the instrument was "properly" made beyond the existence of its recorded trace (REG-KERNEL-001; INV-KERNEL-001).

Where the build cannot satisfy (1) for some in-force regulation, the tightening WOULD narrow s.1 for that record, and the only lawful course is to stop and self-file (PC-16 D7): the cure for that regulation is to record its trace, never to let the kernel strip its floor. I engage the non-narrowing absolute squarely and find it is satisfiable, but only by the carve-out doing real and complete work.

IV. Why I nonetheless reserve the build (Q1, Q3): the floor's own ordering of errors

Holding the interpretive door open is not the same as walking through it now, and here I part company with Position A as a disposition. Between the two ways the kernel can err, the entrenched floor is not neutral. The permissive check errs by OVER-sheltering: at worst a non-constitutive Fatal on a forged regulation is downgraded, a narrow fail-open on a surface the submission concedes is tiny, and one that never touches a genuine record. A tightening errs, if at all, by UNDER-sheltering: narrowing a genuine record's floor. VJS-ACT 10 s.1 is an absolute against the second error and merely tolerant of routed-for-correction defects that flow from the first. Section 1's whole architecture ranks narrowing a genuine record as the graver wrong. So when there is doubt, the floor-faithful direction of error is to over-shelter, which is precisely what the conservative status quo does.

Set against that ordering, the case for building NOW is weak. There is no demonstrated need: no recorded regulation-laundering breach exists; the dangerous order/apex vector is already closed; the residual is a non-constitutive Fatal on a non-order, a class whose Fatals are usually advisory anyway. PC-16 itself reserved this very tightening (D4 as the irreducible remainder, D7 as the narrowing tripwire), and reopened it should be only on a triggering fact, not on the bench's appetite to tidy a residual. The Realm's own discipline against genericising machinery in advance of a demonstrated need (the speclaw-3 test, and REG-KERNEL-001's "only smart enforcement point") points the same way. And the regulation route's reliance on an enactment-record proxy for valid-making, nearer the merits and narrowing lines than PC-16's case, is a reason to build it deliberately against a real fact-pattern rather than speculatively against an imagined one.
