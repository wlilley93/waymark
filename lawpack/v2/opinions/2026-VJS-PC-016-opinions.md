**I. The question, stripped to its constitutional core**

An adversarial audit proved that a staged governed record which merely types the eight characters `assent_source: sovereign_assent`, pointing at no assent event that ever happened, has every Fatal finding downgraded to a route-for-correction Warning and commits. A bench-less, never-convened "Supreme Court order" passed a green validate by this route. The downgrade is performed by `vjs-engine` at `crates/vjs-engine/src/lib.rs:153`, keyed on `front_door::declares_valid_assent`, which (at `front_door.rs:42`) returns true on bare membership of the `VALID_ASSENT_SOURCES` allow-list and nothing more.

The crux the submission frames honestly is this. The floor that performs the downgrade, ACT-ASSENTED-RECORD-PROTECTION (VJS-ACT 10), is entrenched: by its own s.2 it is amendable "only by a Sovereign-assented constitutional Act citing this Act by number." So the tempting engineering fix, "make the kernel block the forgery," walks straight into the entrenched wall. If requiring resolution *narrows* the floor, only the Sovereign may do it, and a bench that did it from its own chair would be legislating ultra vires. The whole matter therefore turns on a single classification: is requiring resolution an **interpretation** of the word "valid," or an **amendment** of the floor?

I hold it is interpretation, squarely and without strain, and I hold so because the entrenched text says so in its own words. I now set out why, question by question.

**II. Q1 - NATURE: "valid assent_source" already requires resolution on the face of the entrenched text**

The submission treats this as an open interpretive question to be reasoned to from s.2's "lacking a valid assent_source." It need not be reasoned to. It is **defined**, expressly, in the constitutional founding Act on which the whole assent regime rests.

ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.23 (the Assent-source rule, an entrenched-grade provision of the founding constitutional Act, settled by [2026] REALM-SC 10) reads:

> "A record claims binding V2 runtime force only if it declares an assent_source **resolving to** a specific Sovereign-assent event (sovereign_assent) or a standing-bounded route **tracing to** specific Sovereign assent (standing_bounded_assent). Absence, emptiness, an unrecognised form, **or an unresolved trace** each cause rejection."

That is not my gloss. That is the enacted definition of a valid assent_source. The term carries resolution **inside itself**. "sovereign_assent" is not the valid thing; it is the *name of a kind* of valid thing, and the Act tells you what makes an instance of that kind valid: it must *resolve to a specific Sovereign-assent event*. An "unresolved trace" is, by the Act's own enumeration, a ground of **rejection** standing shoulder to shoulder with absence and emptiness.

INV-ASSENT-SOURCE-001, the invariant that gives s.23 "continuing deterministic effect," repeats the same words in its remedy: a record "carries [binding force] only if it declares an assent_source **resolving to** a specific Sovereign-assent event ... an **unresolved trace** ... cause[s] rejection." The provenance note even tells the implementer where the test lives and that "missing field and **unresolved trace** both rejected" are to be proof-tested.

So when VJS-ACT 10 s.1 protects a record "that declares a **valid** assent_source" and s.2 withholds all protection from a record "lacking a **valid** assent_source," the word "valid" is not a blank the kernel may fill with whatever is cheapest to compute. It is a term of art already defined by s.23: valid = resolving to a real Sovereign-assent event. A bare allow-listed string pointing at nothing is, in the precise vocabulary of the founding Act, an **unresolved trace**, which "cause[s] rejection."

The kernel's `is_valid_assent_value` therefore does not implement the entrenched term. It implements a **proper subset** of it: the cheap half (is the *name* on the list?) while silently dropping the operative half (does it *resolve*?). The bug, in constitutional terms, is an **under-implementation of an existing entrenched word**. Reading "valid" to mean "resolving" does not add a requirement to the floor; it restores the requirement the founding Act already wrote and the kernel happens not yet to check.

I note, and find it telling, that the kernel's own authors saw this seam coming. `crates/vjs-core/src/scope.rs:72` records, in terms, that the loader keys on the presence of a well-formed authority and never re-adjudicates it, because "**whether the order or assent event truly exists is the separate provenance question reserved to the assent-resolution court**." The codebase already names this court and reserves this very question to it. I am that court, deciding that reserved question. The answer the code reserved a slot for is the answer the text compels: existence (resolution) is required; the allow-list check was only ever the well-formedness half.

**Disposition on Q1:** "Valid assent_source," as the entrenched term is defined by ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.23 and carried into VJS-ACT 10 s.1/s.2, **requires the declared assent to resolve to a real, digest-matched Sovereign-assent record whose scope and date cover the asserting record.** Mere value-on-the-allow-list does not suffice and never did; the allow-list is the necessary-but-not-sufficient first half of the test. Position A is correct on the nature question.

**III. Q2 - MECHANISM: the deterministic, model-free resolution check**

REG-KERNEL-001 and INV-KERNEL-001 are absolute: the kernel is "the only smart enforcement point," it "never deliberates, drafts, or judges merits," and it makes no model calls. A resolution check must therefore be wholly deterministic, mechanical, and fail-closed. It is, and the provenance estate already carries exactly the fields it needs. The genuine assent events under `lawpack/v2/provenance/assent/` each carry `kind: sovereign_assent_event`, a `date`, an `instrument`, an `instrument_digest` (a pinned sha256), and `effects` enumerating what was lodged or entrenched. This is a resolvable, digest-pinned, scoped, dated table.

The check I direct is the following, run at the point of the downgrade decision (the `declares_valid_assent` gate at `vjs-engine/src/lib.rs:153`), and only there. A record earns the floor's protection if and only if, deterministically:

1. **Well-formedness (the existing half).** Its own top-level `assent_source:` field carries a value on the INV-ASSENT-SOURCE-001 allow-list (`sovereign_assent` or `standing_bounded_assent`). `declares_valid_assent` already does this. Fail = no protection, stays Fatal (unchanged).

2. **Resolution (the restored half).** The record must carry a deterministic, machine-checkable **pointer** to a specific assent event in the canon provenance estate, and that pointer must resolve. Concretely: the asserting record must declare the assent it relies on by a key the kernel can look up (e.g. an `assent_event:` id, or an `instrument_digest:` sha256) that loads to exactly one `kind: sovereign_assent_event` record under the pinned `lawpack/v2/provenance/assent/` tree, whose own `instrument_digest` matches the declared digest byte-for-byte. A declared source that names no event, or names one that does not load, or whose digest does not match, is an **unresolved trace** and is treated under s.23 as **no valid assent_source**: the protection does not attach and the finding stays Fatal. For `standing_bounded_assent`, resolution means the declared bounded-route record itself resolves to a specific Sovereign-assent event by the same chain (the "tracing to specific Sovereign assent" of s.23); a standing route that traces to nothing is equally unresolved.

3. **Coverage (scope and date).** The resolved assent event must actually **cover** the asserting record: the asserting record must fall within the event's declared `effects`/scope and not antedate the event's `date`. The substrate already ships the coverage relation: `EntityScope::covers` in `scope.rs:46` is a deterministic prefix-on-the-cascade test. An apex (root) assent covers everything beneath it; a narrower assent covers only its own subtree. A record asserting authority outside the resolved event's scope is, for that assertion, unresolved and unprotected. This closes the subtler forgery of pointing a genuine narrow assent at a record it never authorised.

If, and only if, all three hold, the record declares a valid assent_source within s.23 and the VJS-ACT 10 s.1 floor attaches: its defects are surfaced and routed for correction under code `ASSENTED_ROUTE_FOR_CORRECTION`, never voided or blocked. If any limb fails, the record simply never enters the floor's shelter in the first place, and its findings retain whatever severity they independently carry (Fatal for the bench-less apex order in the audit).

This is pure table lookup, digest comparison, and prefix matching over digest-pinned canon. No model, no merits judgment, no deliberation. It is clerk's work of exactly the kind ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.11 licenses ("record permits and proofs ... resolve authority"). It satisfies REG-KERNEL-001's "only smart point" and "every denial cites the instrument" duties: an unresolved record's denial names s.23 / INV-ASSENT-SOURCE-001 as the instrument that caused it.

**Disposition on Q2:** the deterministic check is well-formed-value AND resolves-to-a-pinned-event AND event-covers-the-record, all over digest-pinned provenance; an unresolved or out-of-scope claim is treated as no valid assent_source and stays Fatal. Build it.

**IV. Q3 - THE FLOOR: requiring resolution protects the floor, it does not amend it**

This is the question on which a faithful judge must be most careful, because the wrong answer here is the one the engineer would most like. I have tested the holding against the entrenched text as hard as I am able, and it survives.

The floor's guarantee in VJS-ACT 10 s.1 is precisely bounded by its own opening words: it protects "a record **that declares a valid assent_source** within the meaning of s.23." The shelter is conditional, and the condition is validity-as-s.23-defines-it. s.2 then states the converse expressly and twice: the Act "confers no force on any record that **lacks a valid assent_source**"; it "protects what the Sovereign has assented to, **it does not enlarge it**."

A record that types `sovereign_assent` while pointing at no assent event has not satisfied the s.1 condition, because under s.23 an unresolved trace is **not a valid assent_source**. Such a record is therefore, by the floor's own terms, a record "lacking a valid assent_source," and s.2 says in so many words that the Act confers it **no force and no protection**. It was never inside the shelter. Requiring resolution does not reach in and remove a protected record; it correctly recognises that the forged record was **never protected to begin with**, because it never met the entrenched condition of entry.

Put the absurdity the other way to see it plainly. The floor exists "to shelter a real assent, never a forgery" (the submission's own framing, and the right one). The assent event of 2026-06-12 records the Sovereign assenting to *this very Act* "to protect **what the Sovereign has assented to**, it does not enlarge it." To read "valid" as bare string-membership is to read the floor as sheltering precisely the forgeries it was enacted to exclude, and to let an agent manufacture the Sovereign's protection by typing a constant. That reading does not honour the entrenched text; it defeats it. A floor that shelters its own counterfeit is not a stronger floor, it is no floor.

Does the never-void/never-block guarantee of s.1 cut the other way, against the kernel ever leaving a Fatal in place on a record that types the magic string? No, because the guarantee operates **on protected records only**, and protection is gated on validity. Leaving the audit's bench-less order Fatal is not "voiding a Sovereign-assented record." It is declining to extend Sovereign protection to a record the Sovereign never assented to. There is nothing to void: there was never any assent. The s.1 prohibition on voiding/blocking is fully respected, because it never bites on a record outside its own entry condition.

I therefore hold that closing the bypass is **interpretation within the Privy Council's power**, and is **not** a constitutive change to the entrenched floor. It changes no word of VJS-ACT 10. It changes no word of s.23. It removes no protection from any genuinely-assented record: every real assent in the canon (the founding adoption, the migration assent, the omnibus, the VJS-ACT 10 assent itself) carries an `instrument_digest` and resolves cleanly, so each remains fully sheltered and each retains its route-for-correction disposition exactly as before. The only records that lose anything are forgeries that were never entitled to anything. That is the floor doing its job, not the floor being amended.

I have asked myself the disciplined entrenchment question the submission rightly forces: if my faithful reading required *adding* a condition the text does not contain, I would be obliged to say so and route it to the Sovereign, and I would. It does not. The condition (resolution) is already in s.23, expressly, as a ground of rejection. I am enforcing an existing entrenched requirement that the kernel under-implements, which is the paradigm case of interpretation, not amendment. To send this to the Sovereign as an "amendment" would itself be an error: it would imply the founding Act does not already require resolution, when it plainly does, and it would leave a known forgery-channel open in the interim on a false premise of incapacity.

**Disposition on Q3:** Requiring resolution does **not** violate s.1's never-void/never-block guarantee, because s.2 already excludes a false claim from the floor's shelter; the forged record was never protected. This is within the Privy Council's interpretive power. It is **not** a constitutive change reserved to the Sovereign. Position A prevails over Position B.

**On Position C (interpretation plus a reserved Sovereign amendment).** C is attractive as belt-and-braces and I have weighed it seriously, because [2026] VJS-PC 14's assent discipline counsels resting entrenched readings on the Sovereign's word where one can. But I decline to *condition* the build on C, for two reasons. First, principle: to draft a Sovereign amendment "confirming" that "valid" means "resolving" risks implying that, absent that amendment, the entrenched word did **not** already require resolution. It did. An amendment to confirm an existing meaning can corrode the meaning it confirms, by suggesting the meaning was contingent on the confirmation. Second, urgency: the bypass is live and proven; an interpretation that is correct on the text should take effect now, not wait on a ceremony it does not need. I therefore adopt **Position A** as the operative holding and build directive, and I adopt the *informational* half of C only: the Sovereign is to be **notified** of this holding (not asked to enact it), so that the Sovereign may, if minded, place the resolution requirement beyond all future argument by an express Act citing VJS-ACT 10 and ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.23 by number. That is the Sovereign's prerogative to exercise or not; it is not a condition of the cure.

**V. Q4 - RESIDUALS: the authority-model findings**

The audit's two companion findings are real and I dispose of them on their own footing, distinct from the assent-resolution holding.

**(a) The agent self-issues its own permits.** `vjs route` mints a permit in one command, so a passing permit proves "an agent chose to route," not "an independent authority approved." The submission says this is by-design under local-sovereign invocation, the agent being the lawful actor, checked by the surfaced flag and the breach duty.

I largely agree, with one tightening. It is constitutionally correct that the agent (Lexby) is the lawful local actor and that routing is an *act of the actor*, not an *approval by a superior*: that is the whole architecture of ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.11 (kernel is clerk, not court) and the federation model of ACT-007. A permit is not meant to be an external approval; it is a recorded, auditable assertion that the actor took the front door. So self-issuance of permits is **by-design and affirmed**. But the permit must not be capable of laundering *assent* or *bench* force the actor does not have, which is the very confusion that produced the audit's bench-less apex order. The tightening is therefore not to forbid self-issuance but to ensure a self-issued permit can never, by itself, satisfy a check that the law reserves to the Sovereign (assent) or to a constituted bench (an order). After this judgment's Q1-Q3 cure, it cannot: assent now requires resolution to a real event the agent cannot manufacture, and the bench-integrity gate (which refused this very order absent a real seat opinion) requires a constituted panel. The permit remains a self-issued act-record; it simply stops being a back-door to powers the actor lacks. I direct that this separation be made explicit in the permit's recorded meaning so the audit trail cannot be read as "approved."

**(b) A kernel-source edit that weakens a gate would pass its own weakened gate.** This is the deeper finding and I will not wave it away as by-design. It is true that an adversarial author with write access to `crates/vjs-core` can weaken `is_valid_assent_value`, rebuild, and the weakened binary will bless the weakening. No purely *internal* deterministic gate can fully close this, because the gate is the thing being edited: a system cannot bootstrap its own integrity against its own author. INV-KERNEL-001 already scopes `crates/vjs-core/**` and `crates/vjs-lawpack/**` as fatal-severity territory, which is the right instinct, but a severity rule in the lawpack is itself enforced by the kernel it guards.

I hold this is **not** wholly by-design and direct a deterministic tightening that raises the cost and the visibility of such an edit without pretending to make it impossible:

- The entrenched-floor logic and the assent-resolution check must be **pinned by digest** the same way the embedded posture pins the lawpack (REG-KERNEL-001: "load only law that hashes to the pinned digest ... fails closed"). A change to the assent-validity code path should require a corresponding, separately-recorded digest bump, so that weakening the gate is at minimum a **visible, recorded, route-for-correction event** that names itself, rather than a silent edit that passes invisibly.
- An edit to the assent-validity or floor code path is itself a change to how an entrenched term (s.23 "valid," VJS-ACT 10 s.1) is enforced. It should therefore surface a **Fatal finding that names ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.14/s.15 and VJS-ACT 10 s.2** (the entrenchment-against-the-machine provisions), flagging that the edit touches entrenched-enforcement code and inviting the breach duty and, where the edit genuinely changes the meaning of an entrenched term, the Sovereign.

Beyond that, I accept the irreducible residue is, correctly, the **breach duty** (s.4-s.8) and the surfaced flag: a sufficiently determined author with kernel write access is outside what any internal machine check can stop, and the constitutional answer to that is the human-accountability layer, not a fantasy of a gate that cannot be edited by the one editing it. The tightening makes the weakening loud and self-naming; it does not, and honestly cannot, make it impossible. I record that limit plainly rather than paper over it.

**Disposition on Q4:** (a) permit self-issuance is **affirmed as by-design**, with an explicit recorded-meaning tightening so a self-issued permit can never read as external approval or carry reserved (assent/bench) force; (b) the self-satisfiable kernel-edit is **tightened** (digest-pin the assent/floor code path, surface an entrenchment-touching Fatal on edits to it) with the candid recognition that the irreducible remainder rests on the breach duty and the surfaced flag, not on any self-enforcing gate.

I have read the case file in full and verified each cited instrument against its source rather than the parties' summary of it. I decide on the pinned bytes alone, with no knowledge of and no regard to the advocate-engineer's preference.

I.  THE QUESTION, STRIPPED TO ITS BONE

An adversarial audit proved that a staged governed record which merely types the four-character-class string `assent_source: sovereign_assent`, pointing at no real assent event, has its Fatal findings downgraded to a route-for-correction Warning and commits. A bench-less, never-convened "Supreme Court order" walked through this door. The parties frame the crux as whether requiring the declared assent to RESOLVE to a real assent record is faithful INTERPRETATION of the entrenched word "valid", or an AMENDMENT of the entrenched floor that only the Sovereign may make.

I hold, for the reasons below, that resolution is not merely a permissible interpretation of "valid": it is the only reading the entrenched text will bear, and the kernel as built is in breach of the text it purports to enforce. I adopt Position A. I reach this on the narrowest possible ground, and I am at pains to say why this is interpretation and emphatically not legislation from the bench.

II.  THE TEXTUAL FINDING THAT DISPOSES OF Q1

The parties, and the framing of the reference, both proceed as though ACT-010 coins "valid assent_source" as a free-standing term whose content the bench must now supply. It does not. I read s1 word for word. It protects:

  "A record that declares a valid assent_source WITHIN THE MEANING OF s.23 (a Sovereign-assented record)".

The entrenched floor does not define validity on its own terms. It expressly incorporates the definition by cross-reference to ACT-COMPUTER-FIRST-REALM s.23. That is dispositive, because s.23 already answers Q1 in terms, and against the engineer's bypass:

  "A record claims binding V2 runtime force only if it declares an assent_source RESOLVING TO a specific Sovereign-assent event (sovereign_assent) or a standing-bounded route TRACING TO specific Sovereign assent (standing_bounded_assent). Absence, emptiness, an unrecognised form, or AN UNRESOLVED TRACE each cause rejection. This is an affirmative allow-list that fails closed; the not-equal-to-self_authorised deny-list form is void as fail-open."

And INV-ASSENT-SOURCE-001, the invariant s.23 names as its continuing deterministic effect, says the identical thing in its own remedy: a record "carries it only if it declares an assent_source RESOLVING TO a specific Sovereign-assent event ... an UNRESOLVED TRACE ... cause[s] rejection."

So the law of this Realm has, since commencement on 2026-06-09 ([2026] REALM-SC 10; Bill 32 assent), defined a valid assent_source as one that RESOLVES. "Valid" has never meant "spelled correctly". The word "resolving" is in the operative text of the entrenched source provision; the word "unresolved ... cause[s] rejection" is in the same sentence. There is nothing for this bench to add to the meaning of "valid". The meaning is already resolution. To read "valid" as mere allow-list membership is not a competing interpretation of the floor: it is a reading that contradicts the floor's own incorporated definition, and it makes the words "resolving to" and "an unresolved trace ... cause rejection" inoperative surplusage. A reading that deletes operative words of an entrenched constitutional section is not available to me.

This is why Position B fails at the threshold. Position B says "valid is value-membership, and requiring resolution is a constitutive change to the entrenched floor." But value-membership is not what the entrenched text says; it is what the kernel CODE does. Position B mistakes the bug for the statute. The Realm did not enact "the string must be on the list." It enacted "the assent_source must RESOLVE." Position B would have me hold that the kernel's present under-implementation IS the entrenched floor, and that bringing the kernel up to the floor's own words requires the Sovereign's assent. That inverts the hierarchy in ACT-008 s10: it elevates a deterministic-clerk implementation defect to constitutional rank and demands a constitutional Act to fix a bug. I reject it.

III.  Q1 DISPOSED

Q1: "VALID assent_source" REQUIRES the declared assent to resolve to a real, digest-matched assent record whose scope and date cover the asserting record. Mere value-on-the-allow-list does NOT suffice. This is the plain meaning of "valid ... within the meaning of s.23", which itself requires "resolving to a specific Sovereign-assent event ... an unresolved trace ... cause[s] rejection." The kernel's `is_valid_assent_value` / `declares_valid_assent` (front_door.rs) and the `AssentSourceValid` predicate (spec.rs lines 514-543) implement only the membership half of a two-part definition and OMIT the resolution half. That omission is the defect. It is a failure to enforce the law, not the law itself.

I record, so the record is candid, that the kernel's own comments KNOW this. The `AssentSourceValid` evaluator in spec.rs recites in its header, verbatim, "or an unresolved trace each cause rejection," and then its body checks only `allowed.iter().any(|a| *a == v)`. The code documents the duty it then fails to perform. That is the cleanest possible proof that resolution is the existing meaning and the gap is implementation, not constitution.

IV.  Q3: THE FLOOR IS NOT TOUCHED, IT IS VINDICATED

Q3 is the constitutional pressure point and I have reasoned hardest here, because the temptation the reference rightly warns against is real: an engineer who wants the bypass closed must not let that wish recruit the bench into amending an entrenched text.

I have tested my reading against s1's never-void / never-block guarantee as an ABSOLUTE, and it survives, for two independent reasons.

First, s2 of the very same entrenched Act is express: "It confers no force on any record that LACKS a valid assent_source; it protects what the Sovereign has assented to, it does not enlarge it." A record that declares `sovereign_assent` but resolves to no assent event LACKS a valid assent_source within the meaning of s.23 (because s.23 says an unresolved trace is rejected). It therefore falls within s2's exclusion, not within s1's shelter. Refusing it is not voiding a protected record; it is declining to extend protection to a record the floor never covered. The floor's guarantee bites, in s1's own words, on "a Sovereign-assented record." A forgery is not a Sovereign-assented record. It is a record that says so and is lying. The entrenched text was written to shelter what the Sovereign actually assented to; reading it to shelter a forgery would be the absurdity the reference itself identifies, and would convert s1 into a laundering instrument by which any agent may immunise any record with one well-typed line. I will not read an entrenched protection of the Sovereign's word into a defeat of the Sovereign's word.

Second, and importantly, the floor's DISPOSITION is untouched. I am not authorising the kernel to VOID or BLOCK anything. The cure here is purely in the antecedent question of WHO is inside the floor. For a record genuinely carrying assent (resolution succeeds), the floor operates exactly as entrenched: every defect is routed for correction, never voided, never blocked. For a record whose assent claim does NOT resolve, the floor never engaged, so the ordinary fail-closed assent rule of s.23 applies and the finding stays Fatal, as it would for any record with no assent_source at all. The forged record is treated identically to the unsigned record, which is right: a false signature is, in law, no signature. Nothing in this requires a record that declares a true, resolving assent to be voided or blocked. The assented-record floor and the ACT-003:s10 / ACT-010 disposition machinery I am bound by ([assented-record-protection] floor; the route-for-correction codes) are left wholly intact for their proper subjects.

Accordingly, requiring resolution does NOT violate s1. It is the means by which s1 is confined to its proper subject (a genuinely assented record) and s2 is given effect (no protection for a record lacking a valid assent_source). This is squarely within the Privy Council's interpretive power. It does not amend the floor; it implements the floor's own incorporated definition. No Sovereign-assented Act citing ACT-010 by number is required, because nothing in ACT-010 (or s.23, or INV-ASSENT-SOURCE-001) is being changed. The instruments already say "resolving"; the kernel will, on these directives, finally do what they say.

I have asked myself the honest contrary question the reference demands: if my faithful reading required AMENDMENT, I would say so and route to the Sovereign, and I would refuse to order the build. It does not. The amendment that would be required is the OPPOSITE one: to make "valid" mean mere membership, someone would have to amend s.23 to delete "resolving to" and "an unresolved trace ... cause rejection." THAT would need the Sovereign. Reading the words that are already enacted needs only this bench.

Position C (declare the interpretation AND draft a reserved Sovereign-assent amendment as belt-and-braces) I considered with respect, because the assent discipline of [2026] VJS-PC 14 is a sound instinct and over-caution toward the Sovereign is rarely a vice. But I decline it, for a reason of constitutional hygiene. To put before the Sovereign an "amendment confirming" a meaning the entrenched text already carries would invite the inference that, absent the Sovereign's confirmation, the meaning was open, which it is not. The cleaner and more honest disposition is to hold plainly that the text already means resolution and to fix the clerk. Where a genuine policy CHANGE to the floor is one day desired (for example, to make resolution stricter than s.23 requires, or to add a new assent form), that is for the Sovereign by an Act citing the relevant provision by number, and I reserve nothing that would prejudice that route.

V.  Q2: THE DETERMINISTIC, MODEL-FREE RESOLUTION CHECK

Resolution must be performed by the kernel as deterministic clerk: no model, no network, no clock-dependence, no semantic similarity (ACT-008 s11; INV-KERNEL-001; REG-KERNEL-001, "the kernel is the only smart enforcement point ... the machinery deterministic"). The materials make this entirely tractable, because genuine assents are already recorded as digest-pinned provenance events that quote the Sovereign's word (lawpack/v2/provenance/assent/*, e.g. 2026-06-12-assented-record-protection-assent.yaml, which carries `kind: sovereign_assent_event`, a `date`, an `instrument`, an `instrument_digest`, and the assented form).

The check, stated deterministically, is: a record's declared `assent_source` confers the floor's protection (and satisfies the assent-source rule) ONLY IF the kernel can bind it to a concrete, pinned assent event in canon that covers the asserting record. Concretely the kernel must, reading structure not raw lines (consistent with the existing `top_level_scalar` discipline):

(1) For `sovereign_assent`: require the asserting record to carry an operative pointer to a specific assent event in lawpack/v2/provenance/assent/ (by id and/or instrument_digest), and require that the named event exist, parse as `kind: sovereign_assent_event`, and carry an `instrument_digest`. An assent claim that names no event, or names an event that does not exist or does not parse as an assent event, is an UNRESOLVED TRACE and is treated as NO valid assent_source: the record is not within the floor and the assent finding stays Fatal.

(2) DIGEST MATCH (the anti-forgery teeth): the assent event's `instrument_digest` must match the digest of the instrument it assents to. An assent is to a specific text (s.14: the Sovereign assents to a digest, "non-presumable"). A record may claim the shelter of an assent event only where that event's pinned digest in fact corresponds to the asserting instrument (or to the instrument from which the asserting record's authority lawfully descends). A claim that resolves to an event whose digest covers some OTHER text does not cover the asserting record and is unresolved as to it.

(3) SCOPE / SUBJECT / DATE COVERAGE: the resolved event must, on its face, cover the asserting record. An assent event lodging Act X (per its `effects` and `instrument`) does not, by its mere existence, assent to unrelated record Y. The kernel checks coverage deterministically from the event's declared `instrument` / `effects` / `date` against the asserting record's identity, and requires the event's date to be not after the asserting record where the record claims to descend from that assent. Anything the kernel cannot deterministically confirm as covering is UNRESOLVED and stays Fatal. The kernel must not "silently choose" coverage (ACT-008 s5): if coverage is unevaluable, it is unresolved, which fails closed.

(4) `standing_bounded_assent`: identical in principle. The declared route must resolve, by pinned trace, to a specific Sovereign-assent event that establishes the standing bound, and the asserting record must fall within that bound's declared scope. An unresolved or out-of-scope standing claim is no valid assent_source.

In all four, the operative rule is the one s.23 already states and the reference proposes: an UNRESOLVED claim is treated as the absence of a valid assent_source, the record is outside the floor, and the finding stays Fatal (fail-closed). A record that DOES resolve keeps the floor's full protection: its remaining, non-assent defects are routed for correction, never voided or blocked. This preserves the entrenched disposition exactly while denying the forgery its shelter.

One caution to the engineer, so the cure does not overreach the floor: resolution governs ONLY whether the assent claim is valid and thus whether the floor engages. It must NOT be wired so that a genuine, resolving assented record can be blocked for some OTHER defect: that would breach s1. The resolution gate fails a record CLOSED only on the ground that its assent does not resolve; for a record whose assent does resolve, the disposition reverts to route-for-correction for everything else. Keep those two questions separate in the code, as ACT-010 keeps them separate in law.

VI.  Q4: THE COMPANION RESIDUALS

(a) The agent self-issues its own permits (`vjs route` mints a permit in one command, proving "an agent chose to route", not "an authority approved").

I hold this is BY DESIGN under the local-sovereign invocation, and I decline to convert the permit into a second approval gate, but I attach one deterministic tightening and a candid limit.

The permit was never a proxy for an external authority's approval. ACT-008 s11 makes the kernel a clerk that may "record permits and proofs"; the permit is the clerk's record that a route was taken and what obligations attach to it, not a sovereign's countersignature. The actual constitutive gate in this Realm is the Sovereign's assent (s.14, "non-automatable, non-presumable, non-delegable"), and for adjudication it is a constituted, convened, odd-numbered bench (s.29; s.15 apex-singleness). Those gates do not, and constitutionally cannot, live in a self-issued permit. So the audit's observation is true but aimed at the wrong artefact: the permit is not where authority is supposed to enter, and demanding that the permit prove "an authority approved" would misunderstand its role. The real defences against a self-serving route are exactly the gates this opinion is reinforcing: the assent floor with resolution (so a permit cannot launder an unassented record into protection), the bench-integrity gate (so a permit cannot conjure a court order without a real, counted bench and a real opinion, which is why this very opinion must be load-bearing), the surfaced route-for-correction flag, and the agent's continuing duty of reasonable skill and care, breach of which is self-fileable.

That said, the audit exposes a real evidentiary weakness: a self-issued permit asserts a route was taken but binds to nothing checkable. The deterministic tightening I direct does not add an approver (there is none to add locally); it makes the permit non-repudiable and scope-honest, so a self-issued permit cannot silently over-reach. See D3.

(b) A kernel-source edit that weakens a gate would pass its own (weakened) gate.

I hold this is the genuine and serious residual, and I do NOT affirm it as merely by-design. It is a self-reference defect: the gate that is supposed to constrain the author cannot constrain an edit to itself, because the edited binary is the thing doing the checking. No purely in-kernel check can fully cure this; a binary cannot bootstrap its own integrity against the hand that compiles it. But "cannot be made perfect" is not "leave it bare." The cure is to move the integrity witness OUTSIDE the mutable surface and to make a weakening edit non-silent, which is consonant with INV-KERNEL-001 and REG-KERNEL-001's "only smart enforcement point ... fails closed" posture and with the entrenchment philosophy of s.14 (entrenched "against the machine"). See D4. I am candid that this raises the cost of a covert weakening and makes it leave tracks; it does not make a determined local author with full write access impossible. Against that author the ultimate backstop is non-machine: the Sovereign and the duty of care. I record that limit rather than pretend it away.

**Q1 - NATURE. Disposition: resolution is required, and this is faithful interpretation of the entrenched term, not amendment of it.**

"VALID assent_source," as ACT-010 s1 uses it, means an assent_source valid within the meaning of s.23, and s.23 means an assent_source **resolving to a specific Sovereign-assent event whose record exists, is digest-pinned, and whose scope and date cover the asserting record**. Mere membership of the two-value allow-list does not suffice; the allow-list names the permitted forms, while s.23 supplies the test (resolution / tracing). A record that declares `assent_source: sovereign_assent` but points at no assent event has, in s.23's own words, an "unresolved trace," which "cause[s] rejection." This is interpretation, not legislation, for a reason stronger than mere plausibility: I am not glossing a silent word. I am applying the very section the entrenched floor names. To read "valid" as bare membership I would have to read s1's words "within the meaning of s.23" out of the statute and read s.23's words "resolving to," "tracing to," and "an unresolved trace ... cause[s] rejection" out of the definition they govern. **Q2 - MECHANISM. Disposition: a deterministic, model-free resolution check, structured as below.**

REG-KERNEL-001 binds the mechanism: the kernel is the only smart enforcement point, it never calls a model, never deliberates, and every denial names its instrument. INV-KERNEL-001 forbids model calls in vjs-core. The check must be pure structure-and-digest arithmetic. It is, and the primitives already exist in the kernel:

For any record that `claims_runtime_force` (or is a governed record under the front door) and declares a top-level `assent_source` on the allow-list, the kernel resolves the declaration as follows, all by `serde_yaml` parse, set membership, and byte-hashing only:

(a) **Existence and form.** There must exist, under `lawpack/v2/provenance/assent/`, an assent record (`kind: sovereign_assent_event`) that the asserting record points at. The pointer is a declared field on the asserting record (an `assent_event:` or equivalent id/path), resolved against the set of known assent-event ids exactly as `IncludedInRuntimeAuthorityGraph` resolves a declared `id` against `all_ids`. A declaration that names no resolvable event is an "unresolved trace" and stays Fatal.

(b) **Digest fidelity.** The asserting record (for a statute, its assented-text digest; for an order, its pinned bytes) must match the digest the assent event pins in its `instrument_digest`. A mismatch is an unresolved trace: the assent on the record's face is not assent to *these* bytes. (c) **Scope and date cover.** The assent event's `effects`/`date` must cover the asserting record: the asserting record must fall within what that event lodged or entrenched, and bear a date not before the event. A record claiming shelter from an assent that did not reach it has, again, an unresolved trace.

If (a), (b), and (c) all hold, the declaration resolves: the record carries a valid assent_source within the meaning of s.23, and the ACT-010 floor applies in full - defects route for correction, never block. If any of (a)-(c) fails, the declaration does **not** resolve; the record is treated as one that **lacks** a valid assent_source; the underlying finding keeps its native severity (Fatal stays Fatal); and the denial names s.23 / INV-ASSENT-SOURCE-001 / ACT-010 s2 as REG-KERNEL-001 requires. No clock, no network, no model: parse, resolve-against-set, hash-compare. Lawful under INV-KERNEL-001 and REG-KERNEL-001.

**Q3 - THE FLOOR. Disposition: requiring resolution does NOT violate s1's never-void/never-block guarantee; s2 already excludes a false claim from the floor's shelter; and this is squarely within the Privy Council's interpretive power, requiring no Sovereign amendment.**

The floor's guarantee in s1 is absolute and I treat it as an absolute: a record that declares a valid assent_source within the meaning of s.23 may never be voided, excluded, or blocked; its defects are always routed for correction. I would strike down any check that voided or blocked a record that genuinely carries assent. The assented-record floor exists precisely so that the machine can never silence the Sovereign's word over a defect, and that protection is sacred. I will not erode it by a hair.

But s1's shelter is, by its own opening words, available only to a record "that declares a valid assent_source within the meaning of s.23." A record bearing an unresolved string has, by Q1, no valid assent_source within s.23. It is therefore, by s2's own words, a record to which the Act "confers no force." Keeping such a record at its native Fatal severity does not pierce the floor; it declines to extend the floor to a record the floor never covered. The Sovereign assented to protect what the Sovereign assented to. The Sovereign did not assent to protect what the Sovereign did not.

Is this interpretation or constitutive change? It is interpretation, and I am candid about where the line sits because the bench was rightly warned not to legislate. A constitutive change would be one that altered what the entrenched text *says*: e.g. narrowing the floor so that a genuinely-assented record could be blocked, or adding a new precondition the text does not contain. I take the two words s1 already uses ("within the meaning of s.23"), apply the section they name exactly as that section is written, and hold that the protected class is, and always was, the class of records whose assent resolves. The class is unchanged. What changes is only the kernel's faithfulness to it: the binary stops reading "valid" as a synonym for "allow-listed," a reading the entrenched text never bore. Correcting a binary that under-enforces an entrenched definition is the ordinary work of a court construing that definition. It needs no Sovereign amendment, because it takes nothing from the Sovereign's text and adds nothing to it.

I have considered, and reject, Position C's belt-and-braces. C would have me build the resolution check **and** draft a void-first-draft Sovereign amendment confirming the reading. I decline the amendment limb, for a reason of constitutional hygiene. To send the Sovereign an amendment "confirming" that "valid" means resolution would imply that, absent the Sovereign's fresh word, "valid" might mean bare membership - it would treat the faithful reading of an existing entrenched section as if it were a gap needing the Sovereign to fill. That concedes the very ground I have just held. The entrenched definition is already in s.23, already Sovereign-assented (Bill 32, 2026-06-09). The court's duty is to apply it, not to ask the Sovereign to re-enact it. Asking for a confirming amendment where the text already governs would dilute, not fortify, assent discipline: it would invite the inference that an entrenched section means nothing until a court procures a redundant assent. I will not set that precedent.

I therefore adopt **Position A: require resolution, as interpretation.** I reject Position B (value-suffices) as the reading that, contrary to its own claim, amends the entrenched text by nullifying s.23's resolution verbs. I reject Position C's reserved-amendment limb as constitutionally unhygienic; its build limb is subsumed in A.

One guardrail I make express, lest the engineer over-read this opinion. The resolution check is a **gate on which records the floor shelters, not a new power to block assented records.** Where a record's assent **does** resolve under Q2, every defect in it - an inert kernel_effect, an unimplemented operation, an s.5 discrepancy - continues to route for correction and may never be blocked or voided. The floor stands at full height for real assent. The check does one thing only: it stops a record that carries **no** real assent from borrowing the floor it was never owed.

**Q4 - RESIDUALS. Disposition: residual (a) is tightened by Q1-Q3 themselves at the point that matters and otherwise affirmed as by-design with one surfacing duty; residual (b) is tightened deterministically.**

**(a) The agent self-issues its own permits (`vjs route` mints a permit in one command).** I affirm this as by-design under the Federation and Local Sovereignty Act (ACT-007) and the local-sovereign invocation, with one qualification and one correction of emphasis.

The permit is not, and was never, a proof that "an authority approved." It is a scoped, recorded, fail-closed **routing record**: it proves that a governed write was routed through the kernel chokepoint, named its scope, and is auditable - REG-FRONT-DOOR-001's purpose. Under ACT-007 s1-s3 the local Principal is the lawful actor in the local jurisdiction; the agent acting on the Principal's invocation IS that actor for routine, reversible, in-scope writes. Requiring a separate human approval for every permit would convert the kernel from clerk to gatekeeper-of-merits, which s.11 (kernel is clerk, not court) forbids, and would defeat the autonomous operation the realm is built for. So for the ordinary case, self-issue is correct and adequately checked by the surfaced, scoped, auditable permit plus the continuing breach duty (ACT-003).

The qualification is the one this very reference closes. The danger the audit actually exhibited was not self-issued *permits*; it was a self-declared *assent* laundering Fatal findings. That danger is killed at its root by Q1-Q3: after the resolution check, no permit and no self-declaration can downgrade a Fatal finding by naming an assent that does not resolve. The class of acts where "an agent chose to route" is dangerously close to "an authority approved" is exactly the class touching the assent floor and the apex - and those are now gated by resolution, not by a typed string. I therefore decline to re-architect the permit into a two-party approval for routine writes, and direct only that the permit record continue to surface, in plain terms, that it is agent-routed self-issue and not authority-approval, so the audit trail never reads as more than it is.

**(b) A kernel-source edit that weakens a gate would pass its own (weakened) gate.** This I do not affirm as merely by-design; it is a genuine self-satisfiability hole and I direct it tightened, deterministically and within the realm's constraints.

The principle is INV-ENTRENCHED-GATE-001 / s.14 / s.15: the entrenched gates are "entrenched against the machine," amendable only by Sovereign-assented constitutional Act citing them by number; the legislature and the machine "may not amend the assent rule" and "may not expand [their] own competence." A kernel edit that weakens the assent-resolution check, the bench gate, the apex-singleness check, or any entrenched-gate enforcement is, in substance, a machine amending an entrenched gate - void ab initio under s.14 whatever a weakened binary then says. The cure is to make the kernel **detect** that it is being asked to weaken its own entrenched enforcement, model-free:

A change to the enumerated entrenched-enforcement surface (the files implementing the assent-resolution check, the bench-integrity gate, the apex-singleness check, the entrenched-gate evaluators) is a governed change of a special class. Its permit/route is **not** self-satisfiable: it routes to a **court** (this Privy Council or above for the entrenched gates), and the kernel treats a write to that surface, absent a citation to a resolving Sovereign-assented amendment or a Privy-Council-or-above order, as a Fatal finding that does **not** downgrade (because, post-Q3, no self-declared assent can downgrade it). This is the same chokepoint discipline the front door already runs, pointed at the kernel's own entrenched enforcement files. It does not make the binary unable to be edited; it makes an edit that **weakens an entrenched gate** unable to pass without the court or the Sovereign the entrenched text already requires. Self-satisfaction is broken because the gate that judges the edit is not the edited copy: the check keys on *which surface is being written and whether the write cites resolving authority*, and a weakened copy still leaves that routing fact true.

I am candid that no purely-local binary can be made perfectly tamper-proof against an author with full write access who simply deletes the check and recompiles; the realm's ultimate backstop there is the breach duty (ACT-003), the surfaced audit record, and, for subscribers, the digest-pinned embedded posture (REG-KERNEL-001) that loads only canon hashing to the pinned lock. What I direct is that the realm not leave the hole **open by design** when a deterministic chokepoint closes the ordinary case. Tighten it as above.
