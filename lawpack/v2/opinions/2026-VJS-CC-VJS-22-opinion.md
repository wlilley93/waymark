# [2026] VJS-CC-VJS 22

**County Court, single judge. On own motion, on a symmetric case file.**

I note at the outset the oddity of my position. I sit by the authority of
`2026-VJS-COURTS-CONSTITUTION-001` D1, and the question is whether that instrument is
defective. If it is void I have no seat and cannot say so; if it is sound I may. A court
cannot saw off the branch it sits on, and I mention it not as an excuse but because it
disposes of one of the three positions before argument. **Position A cannot be right in
its strong form**: an instrument whose invalidity would unmake every bench in the realm,
including the one hearing the challenge, is not something a subordinate court can find
void. That is not deference. It is arithmetic.

## The question is which rule, not which exemption

The case file offers a foundational-instruments carve-out (B) and a not-an-order reading
(C). I take neither, because both accept the framing I think is wrong: that the apex-bench
rule reaches this record and something must excuse it.

Read the rule as it is actually written. SC 2 holds that "**no V2 court may issue an order
until so constituted**". The subject of that sentence is a COURT, and its object is the
ISSUING of an order. It is a rule about the capacity of a body to act.

Now ask what `2026-VJS-COURTS-CONSTITUTION-001` is. It is not a V2 court issuing an order.
It is the instrument by which V2 courts come to exist at all, deriving its force from
Sovereign assent and the V1 handover of [2026] REALM-SC 10 - both of which the record
declares on its face. At the moment it took effect there was no V2 bench to name, and that
is not an accident of drafting. **It is the content of the instrument.**

So the rule never reached it, and the reason is not that constituting instruments are
special. It is that the rule is about courts, and this is not a court.

That is Position C's conclusion. I do not adopt Position C's reasoning, which says lodging
it as an `order` is "a filing convention" - the case file is right that the record carries
`court:`, `holding:` and `directives:` and is relied on as binding, and I will not hold
that a record is not the kind of thing it plainly is. The point is narrower and survives
that objection: a record may be an order in every formal sense and still not be **a court
issuing an order**, where what it does is create the courts.

## The boundary, which is the part that matters

The case file is right that the danger is not the outcome but the reasoning. "Exempt
because foundational" is exactly what the author of any bench-less order would say, and an
exemption a gate cannot check is an invitation. So I state the boundary in a form a gate
can hold, and I direct that the gate hold it rather than trusting anyone to.

**A record escapes the apex-bench requirement only where the record itself is what
constitutes the bench that would otherwise be required.** Two things follow and both are
machine-checkable:

1. It must be the instrument the kernel resolves as `COURTS_CONSTITUTION_ID` - the
   identity the bench verifier already reads to check every other order. Not a class, not
   a category, not a self-declared kind. The one record every bench check already depends
   on.
2. It must derive its force from outside the court system it constitutes: an
   `assent_source` resolving to Sovereign assent. A body cannot bootstrap its own
   authority, and this instrument does not claim to - it points at REALM-SC 10 and at
   assent.

An ordinary order that omitted its bench satisfies neither limb. It is not the
constitution, and its force comes from the bench it failed to name. **The carve-out cannot
be borrowed, because borrowing it would require being the constitution.**

## What I do not decide

Whether a FUTURE constituting instrument - a successor constitution, or a foreign
subscriber's - takes the same exemption. The limbs above would reach it, and that may well
be right, but no such instrument is before me and the question of what happens when two
constitutions exist at once is not one to answer on a record where it does not arise.

I also do not disturb the correction register's treatment of anything else. This is one
record and one rule.

## On how this was found

Worth recording because it is the second time in two days. The defect was invisible for as
long as the rule ran only over records somebody was touching, and this record was lodged
once and never re-staged. It surfaced within hours of the at-rest sweep landing
([2026] VJS-CC-VJS 20 D13). An unrun gate banks debt, and what it banked here was not an
error in the record but an error in the RULE - a rule that had never once been asked of the
instrument it cites as its own authority.

That is the more interesting failure. A gate quoting an instrument it has never tested
against is asserting a relationship nobody checked.
