# [2026] VJS-CC 15 - The s.5(a) kernel-effect teeth-gate (operational limb)

**County Court of the Vibe Justice System, single judge (odd bench of 1) per 2026-VJS-COURTS-CONSTITUTION-001 D1. Convened on the registrar's own motion. Before: Marrowby CCJ. Disposition: operational limb disposed; the limb-(a) kernel-effect gate is MACHINERY WITH CONDITIONS; permission to appeal GRANTED to the Privy Council (bench of 3) on the dividing-line and assent-floor points.**

I sit as Marrowby CCJ, single judge of the County Court of the Vibe Justice System, convened on the registrar's own motion. The registrar's preference is not before me; I decide on the symmetric file alone.

The question is whether a deterministic predicate enforcing s.5 limb (a) is kernel machinery within my subordinate competence, or whether mechanically defining what "has kernel effect" means touches the runtime-authority-graph constitution and is reserved. I take the dividing-line ratio of Ledgerward CCJ in [2026] VJS-CC-AGENT-UNIVERSE-V2 14 as binding and on all fours: an instrument is machinery where it administers or records and does not void a constitutive act; it is reserved where it re-defines what the constitutive thing IS.

I confront the sharp tension at once, and I do not minimise it. The order-schema instrument in Ledgerward was a recording duty, and was saved because it voided nothing. The gate before me is not a recording duty. As proposed in fact (2), it FAILS records and excludes them from the runtime authority graph. On its face that is a validity gate, and validity gates over constitutive acts are the reserved side of the line. If the analysis stopped there, this would escalate.

But it does not stop there, and the distinction that saves it is the one the file itself isolates. The exclusion of an inert record is not the kernel's invention. s.5 is primary law, already assented, and s.5 ITSELF declares that a record with no kernel effect is excluded. The kernel does not legislate that exclusion; it gives deterministic effect to an exclusion the Sovereign has already enacted. That is the concordance competence under which the witness-soundness sweep lawfully proceeded: code made to do what the statute already says, not code that says something new. To that extent the gate is machinery of the purest kind, because s.5 is today the one diagnostic standard in the kernel with no teeth, and s.5's own words, "the kernel must not silently choose," are a standing instruction to build exactly this.

The danger lives not in enforcing exclusion but in the MECHANISM of judging effect: a 37-verb allowlist. Here the assent-floor objection bites, and I engage it directly. A record the Sovereign assented to (s.23) might declare a kernel_effect using a verb not yet implemented in the registry. Such a record is NOT inert; it has effect the Sovereign willed. To FAIL and exclude it would be the computational legislature narrowing what counts as valid law by the contingency of what the registry happens to implement, expanding its own competence against the entrenched gate of s.14 and the non-presumable assent floor of s.23. That cannot be built as repo-local machinery, and if the gate's only disposition were exclusion, I would reserve.

I do not reserve, because the objection is defusible without remainder, and the cure is dictated by s.5's own grammar. s.5 supplies TWO dispositions: an inert record is excluded, but a discrepancy is "a validation defect routed for correction." The unimplemented-verb case is precisely a discrepancy, not inertia: the record HAS declared effect; the kernel merely cannot yet evaluate it. So the lawful disposition is to ROUTE FOR CORRECTION, never to void. An assented record using an unimplemented verb is flagged as a registry-coverage gap, and the cure is to extend the registry, not to remove the record. The allowlist is thereby not a closed validity ceiling that the legislature has narrowed; it is an editable administrative surface, and silent voiding, the very thing s.5 forbids, never occurs. With that disposition the gate voids no constitutive act, narrows no assent, and creates no force by computation. It administers s.5. It is machinery, with conditions.

That said, the line I have drawn, between deterministic effect to a primary-law exclusion and constitutive re-definition of "kernel effect," and the allowlist's brush against the assent floor, are matters of general constitutional importance fit for three. I grant permission on those points while disposing the operational limb now.

The order will carry:

D1. The limb-(a) gate may be built as kernel machinery, enforcing s.5's exclusion by giving it deterministic effect, subject to D2 to D5.

D2. A runtime-force record whose declared kernel_effect binds to no evaluable arm of the registry is ROUTED FOR CORRECTION as an s.5 validation defect; it is never silently excluded or voided.

D3. Where such a record carries a resolving assent_source (s.23), it is additionally flagged as a registry-coverage gap; the cure is registry extension, not record removal.

D4. The gate parses declared kernel_effect structure only, calls no model, uses no semantic similarity, and leaves limb (b) divergence at the agent rung (s.11).

D5. Permission to appeal to the Privy Council is GRANTED on the dividing-line and assent-floor points; the operational limb is disposed and the build may proceed under D1 to D4 pending any appeal.
