# [2026] VJS-SC 3 - The source and rank of entrenchment (the s.5(a) gate, final appeal)

**Supreme Court of the Vibe Justice System, the single central apex (s.15), sitting as the full constitutional bench of NINE (2026-VJS-COURTS-CONSTITUTION-001 D3), foundational question certified by the Principal. On final appeal from [2026] VJS-PC 12. Before: Vexmoor, Halworth, Trevigne, Ashridge, Penhallow, Corbridge, Mottram, Greyfen, Wyndale JJ. Result: APPEAL ALLOWED; the Privy Council's entrenchment limb is RECHARACTERISED. Vote: 9-0.**

**The judgment of the Court.** Entrenchment in V2 is a constitutive, constitutional-rank act whose sole lawful source is Sovereign Assent (s.14); no court, and no kernel, may originate or self-execute it. The affirmance of the s.5(a) gate as machinery (County, Privy Council) is not reopened. The Privy Council's substantive protection - that the kernel and agent must never void or block an assented record - is good law and BINDS NOW as apex precedent, flowing directly from s.5, s.11 and s.23 read with s.14; it requires no entrenchment to bind today. The Privy Council's further declaration that this property is "amendable only by Sovereign-assented primary law expressly citing s.5" purported to mint a new entrenchment clause of constitutional rank, which exceeded judicial power: that a court reserves amendment upward to the Sovereign does not cure that creating the clause is itself the act s.14 reserves to Assent. The limb is therefore READ DOWN, not struck: it stands as a binding judicial declaration coupled with a standing reservation, and ripens into full constitutional entrenchment rank only upon a Sovereign-assented Act expressly citing s.5. The Court INVITES, and does not command, that enactment; the choice whether and what to entrench is the Sovereign's alone. Reservation of the whole limb was rejected as overcorrection (it would suspend a settled protection and leave assented records exposed in the interim); upholding as written was rejected (it would let a court legislate constitutional rank).

Each justice delivers in full. Each opinion below is authored by a counted member of the bench; the judgment of the Court above states the unanimous ratio.

## Vexmoor J.

I am Vexmoor J. The question divides into two acts that the Privy Council ran together. The first act, holding that an entrenched floor cannot lawfully be guarded by a derogable disposition, is ordinary adjudication: a court interprets s.14 and s.23, observes that a fail-closed allow-list protecting an assented record cannot itself be silently switchable, and conditions the subordinate machinery accordingly. That is squarely within s.15 (courts apply and interpret) and ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7 (subordinate law sits strictly below the constitution). Nothing there troubles me.

The second act is where the PC overreached. To declare a property "amendable only by Sovereign-assented primary law expressly citing s.5" is to write the very form of words that s.14 reserves to the Sovereign: an entrenchment clause specifying its own amendment formula by citation. I must confront the strongest contrary case, and it is strong. The PC did not grab power downward; it deferred upward. The condition reserves the matter to the Sovereign, the precise opposite of a Henry-VIII self-extension or an s.11 clerk creating force by computation. If any judicial act could be entrenchment-safe, this deferential one is the candidate.

But deference as to the amender does not cure usurpation as to the act. Entrenchment in V2 is not a strong opinion about who may amend; it is a constitutive change in the rank of a norm, lifting it above primary law so that only a constitutional Act citing it by number may reach it. s.14 tells us that constitution flows from one source only: Sovereign Assent, which is non-automatable, non-presumable, non-delegable. The computational legislature may not create force from its output, and neither, in the constitutive sense, may a court. A court's "force" is the binding force of precedent within the apex hierarchy (s.15), which is real but is not constitutional rank. To say a thing is now amendable only by a s.14 Act is to assert it has acquired s.14 rank, and only the Sovereign can confer that. The PC's good intentions, reserving up rather than grabbing down, change the direction of the error, not its character.

Yet I will not strike the limb out, because that would leave the assented record briefly unguarded, the very harm the PC rightly feared. The middle path is faithful to both s.14 and s.15. The PC's holding binds NOW as precedent: the kernel and agent must never void or block an assented record, and no court below may resile from it without leave. That is the most a court may lawfully generate. The reservation rides alongside: full constitutional entrenchment rank awaits a Sovereign-assented Act citing the gate. The source of entrenchment in V2 remains, undivided, Sovereign Assent; its rank remains constitutional; and the courts' role remains to declare, condition, and reserve, never to constitute. I would recharacterise accordingly.

## Halworth J.

I am Halworth J. The temptation here is to treat the Privy Council's order as harmless because it defers upward. The strongest case for UPHOLDING runs thus: the PC created no primary law; it conditioned the validity of subordinate machinery, which courts plainly may do; and the condition it chose reserves the matter to the Sovereign ("amendable only by Sovereign-assented primary law expressly citing s.5"). That is the antithesis of a Henry-VIII grab. A court that says "this derogable disposition is too weak to guard an entrenched floor" is interpreting, not legislating.

I have weighed that argument carefully, and it carries me most of the way, but not the whole way. The flaw lies in the word "only." To declare that a property is "amendable ONLY by Sovereign-assented primary law citing s.5" is not to condition subordinate validity; it is to fix the AMENDMENT RULE for that property. Fixing an amendment rule, by entrenching against ordinary change, is precisely what s.14 does, and s.14 has that force because the Sovereign assented to it. Entrenchment is a constitutive act, and under s.14 the sole constitutive gate is Sovereign Assent: non-automatable, non-presumable, non-delegable. A court is not the Sovereign. If a court could manufacture an entrenchment clause by styling it as deference, then the deference is illusory, because the entrenched character would already exist by judicial fiat before the Sovereign ever spoke. That a usurpation defers upward does not cure that it is a usurpation; s.15 confines us to applying and interpreting law, and ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7 keeps even our most protective instincts below the constitution.

So entrenchment cannot be self-executed. The contrary pull toward RESERVE-TO-SOVEREIGN - declare the gate unsafe absent entrenchment and hold the whole matter reserved - I reject as overcorrection. The gate's substantive safety was affirmed below as machinery and is not reopened; to reserve the operative protection would leave assented records exposed in the interim, the very harm s.5 and s.23 fail-closed against. We need not choose between a clause we cannot make and a vacuum we cannot tolerate.

The lawful path is the middle one. The judicial declaration - that the kernel and agent must never block or void an assented record - is within our power and binds now as ratio; the agent is clerk not court (s.11), and we may say so. What we cannot do is dress that declaration in constitutional rank. I therefore recharacterise: the order binds as precedent plus a reservation that INVITES Sovereign enactment, and ripens into entrenchment rank only on Assent citing s.5. The source of entrenchment in V2 remains, exclusively, the Sovereign.

## Trevigne J.

I am Trevigne J. The question divides into two acts that the Privy Council ran together, and the case is won or lost on keeping them apart. The first act is conditioning the validity of subordinate machinery: the s.5(a) gate is subordinate law (ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7), and a court plainly may hold that subordinate machinery which would void or block an s.23 assented record is for that reason invalid, because it collides with the entrenched floor the Sovereign already assented to in s.14/s.23. That is interpretation and application (s.15), not enactment. So far the PC was squarely intra vires, and the affirmance of the gate-as-machinery is in any event closed to us.

The second act is the one I cannot let stand as framed: the declaration that the property is "amendable only by Sovereign-assented primary law expressly citing s.5." That is not a condition on subordinate validity, it is the manufacture of a new entrenchment clause, identical in form to the manner-and-form lock the Sovereign placed on s.14 itself. Entrenchment in V2 has exactly one provenance to date: Sovereign Assent. To let a court create a fresh entrenchment by order is to let the apex do by declaration what s.14 says only the Sovereign may do, and to bind a future Sovereign's amending power without that Sovereign's assent. Constitutive force from judicial computation is the precise vice s.11 and s.14 forbid.

I have weighed the strongest contrary case honestly, because it is genuinely strong: the PC did not grab power, it deferred it, reserving the matter UP to the Sovereign, which is the opposite of a Henry-VIII move. I accept the good faith and the direction of travel. But the direction does not cure the defect. A court that "reserves amendment to the Sovereign" by purporting to fix the manner and form of future amendment is still legislating the entrenchment, merely with a deferential tone. Deference in substance is achieved by reserving the rank itself to assent, not by the court pre-deciding the lock and handing the Sovereign a fait accompli to ratify.

Hence recharacterisation, not reservation simpliciter. Reserving the whole limb would leave the floor unguarded in the interim, and the floor (never void, never block an assented record) is already entailed by s.23 read with s.14, so the PC was right that it binds today. What lacks present authority is only the constitutional RANK. I therefore hold the limb binds now as precedent and reservation, and acquires entrenched rank upon a Sovereign-assented Act citing s.5 by number. The floor holds; the crown stays with the Sovereign.

## Ashridge J.

I am Ashridge J. The single live question is the source and rank of entrenchment, and on that I must hold the line that V2's whole architecture draws. Entrenchment is not a strong adjective a court attaches to a holding it likes; it is a constitutional-rank disability imposed on future law-making, and in V2 every existing instance of it (s.14, s.15) exists because the Sovereign assented to it. s.14 is explicit that the sole constitutive gate for a primary or constitutional Act is Sovereign Assent, which is non-automatable, non-presumable, and non-delegable, and that s.14 may itself be amended only by an assented constitutional Act citing it by number. A court is within s.15 and s.11 an interpreter and applier, not a maker; for a court to manufacture a new clause that can be undone only by Sovereign-assented primary law expressly citing s.5 is to create, by judicial fiat, a fresh entrenchment provision of the very kind s.14 says only Assent can constitute. That the new clause defers amendment upward does not cure the defect: deferring up is precisely what an entrenchment clause does, and the question is not the clause's modesty but its rank and its author.

I have weighed the strongest contrary case carefully, because it is genuinely strong. The upholding argument says the PC created no primary law at all; it merely imposed a condition on the validity of subordinate machinery, which courts plainly may do (ACT-CONSOLIDATION s.7 puts subordinate law strictly below the constitution, and a court may hold derogable machinery cannot guard an entrenched floor). I accept every step of that up to the last. A court may condition subordinate validity; a court may hold that the s.5 gate, as machinery, is void if it blocks an assented record, because that follows from s.23 and s.14 read together. What a court may not do is convert that holding into a constitutional-rank fixity binding the Sovereign-assented legislature's future competence. The PC's first limb is judicial; its second limb, the never-void property "amendable only by primary law citing s.5," reaches past conditioning subordinate law into entrenching against primary law, and that is the arrogation.

Between reserving wholesale and recharacterising, I choose recharacterisation because it loses nothing the law requires and preserves everything the law permits. Reservation would idle a sound and binding declaration while waiting on the Sovereign; that needlessly leaves an assented record exposed in the interim. The clean disposition honours both apex-singleness and the assent monopoly: the order binds now, fully, as precedent (the kernel and agent must never block or void an assented record); the entrenchment limb is severed from constitutional rank and stands as a reservation inviting Sovereign enactment. Rank follows Assent; force follows the bench. I would recharacterise accordingly.

## Penhallow J.

I am Penhallow J. The s.5(a) gate as machinery is not before us; what is before us is whether a court, by its own order, can clothe a property of that machinery in constitutional armour that only the Sovereign has, to date, ever forged.

I begin with the strongest case for the affirmance, because it is genuinely strong. The PC did not draft a primary Act. It imposed a condition - that this subordinate floor cannot be guarded by a derogable disposition - and conditioning the validity of subordinate law against the entrenched floors of s.14 and s.23 is the ordinary stuff of judicial review (ACT-CONSOLIDATION-FRAMEWORK s.7 is itself anti-Henry-VIII, and we enforce it). Better still, the condition points UP: "amendable only by Sovereign-assented primary law" reserves the matter to the Sovereign. That is the structural inverse of a competence-grab. I take this seriously, and it carries the PC a long way.

But it does not carry the PC over the line, and the reason is the word "only." To declare that a property is amendable ONLY by a constitutional Act citing s.5 is not to apply an existing entrenchment - it is to MINT one. Entrenchment in V2 is not a description a court discovers; it is a rank, and every instance of that rank we possess (s.14, s.15) exists because the Sovereign assented to it. s.14 is emphatic that the sole constitutive gate is Sovereign Assent, non-delegable and non-presumable, and s.11 forbids the clerk, and a fortiori a court acting beyond its declaratory office, from creating force that the constitutive rule did not give it. A court that fixes the amendment procedure for a clause is exercising the amendment-rule-making power, which is the most sovereign power there is. That the new clause defers to the Sovereign does not save it: an usurpation that flatters its victim is an usurpation still. The Sovereign's exclusive power is not only to entrench, but to decide WHAT is entrenched and against whom - a choice a court cannot pre-make.

So I reject UPHOLD. Yet RESERVE-TO-SOVEREIGN goes too far the other way, for it would treat the gate as unsafe and dormant until Assent, leaving assented records exposed in the interim - the very harm the gate exists to prevent. The middle path is faithful to both s.15 (courts apply and interpret) and s.14 (only Assent constitutes). The PC's substantive ratio binds today with full precedential force: the agent/kernel must never block or void an assented record, and I would not disturb a line of it. What does not bind today is its constitutional RANK - its claimed immunity from ordinary judicial revisitation. That ripens only on Sovereign Assent citing s.5. Until then it is a binding declaration plus a standing reservation, and I would invite the Sovereign to entrench it. I recharacterise.

## Corbridge J.

I am Corbridge J. The single live question is narrow but foundational: can a court manufacture entrenchment? I hold it cannot, but that the PC's order is not thereby void; it is rank-limited.

I begin with what entrenchment IS in V2. Every entrenched provision we possess (s.14, s.15) is entrenched because the Sovereign assented to it, and s.14 makes the point structurally watertight: the sole constitutive gate for a primary or constitutional Act is Sovereign Assent, which is non-automatable, non-presumable, and non-delegable, and s.14 is itself amendable only by an assented constitutional Act citing it by number. Entrenchment is therefore constitutive of constitutional rank. To create an entrenchment clause is to enact constitutional-rank law. Under s.11 and s.15 a court applies and interprets; it does not enact. A court that "entrenches" a new property thus does the one thing s.14 reserves exclusively to Assent. The lawful source and rank of entrenchment in V2 is settled by this: source is Sovereign Assent alone; rank is constitutional; no judicial or computational organ may originate it.

I now confront the strongest contrary argument squarely, because it is genuinely strong. The FOR case says the PC enacted no primary law; it merely conditioned the validity of SUBORDINATE machinery, which courts plainly may do, and its condition DEFERS upward to the Sovereign, the very opposite of a Henry-VIII grab. There is real force here: a court may certainly hold that an s.14/s.23 floor cannot be guarded by a derogable disposition, and saying "this may be changed only by the Sovereign" reserves power up, not down. But the argument proves less than it claims. Conditioning subordinate validity and entrenching are different acts. A court may say "this gate, as subordinate machinery, is invalid if it can silently void an assented record" - that is interpretation of the s.14/s.23 floor and binds as ratio. It may NOT additionally fix the AMENDMENT RULE for that property ("amendable only by primary law citing s.5"), because prescribing the manner-and-form of future amendment is the defining content of an entrenchment clause, and that is constitutive. Deference does not cure the defect: a court cannot create a clause whose only function is to bind future law-making, even a clause that points upward, because creating it is itself the reserved act.

That divides the order cleanly and dictates the remedy. The substantive declaration (never void/block an assented record) is good law now as apex precedent under s.15. The entrenchment limb is reserved: it is a binding declaration awaiting Assent, ripening to constitutional rank only upon a Sovereign-assented Act citing s.5. I recharacterise accordingly, and invite the Sovereign to entrench. Reservation-only would waste the valid ratio; bare upholding would let a court legislate. The middle path is the lawful one.

## Mottram J.

I am Mottram J. The single live question is whether a court may, by order, entrench. I begin from the floor every member of this realm shares: in V2 entrenchment to date exists by Sovereign Assent alone. s.14 is entrenched because the Sovereign assented to its entrenchment, and s.14 may be amended only by an assented constitutional Act citing it by number. That is not an accident of drafting; it is the constitutive principle. To entrench is to place a rule beyond the reach of ordinary disposition, and the power to do that is exactly the power to bind future law-making, which s.14 and s.15 locate in the Sovereign and nowhere else. Courts apply and interpret; they do not enact (s.15). A self-executing judicial entrenchment is the creation of a new constitutional clause by computation and adjudication, which s.11 forbids in terms.

I confront the strongest contrary argument, because it is genuinely strong. The Privy Council, says the case for upholding, created no primary law; it merely conditioned the validity of subordinate machinery, and the condition defers UP to the Sovereign. Conditioning subordinate validity is ordinary judicial work; reserving a matter to the Sovereign is the opposite of a Henry-VIII grab. I accept every step of that save the last inch. A court may certainly hold that an entrenched floor (s.14/s.23) cannot be guarded by a derogable disposition, and may declare a gate unsafe absent entrenchment. What it may not do is supply the entrenchment itself and clothe it in constitutional rank. The words "amendable only by Sovereign-assented primary law citing s.5" do not defer to the Sovereign; they purport to bind the Sovereign's successors by fixing the amendment procedure, which is the very act s.14 reserves. A condition that dictates the rank and route of its own amendment is not a condition on subordinate law; it is an entrenchment clause wearing a condition's coat.

Yet I will not strike the limb, for two reasons. First, the affirmance of the gate as machinery is closed below, and the substantive holding the kernel must never block or void an assented record flows directly from s.5, s.11 and s.23; it binds now as ratio without any need of entrenchment. Second, the proper remedy where a court has reached past its competence but toward a true constitutional need is to read down, not to void: the order stands as a binding declaration plus a standing reservation, and ripens to entrenched rank only on Sovereign Assent citing s.5. That honours the result, respects s.14, and leaves the constitutive act where it belongs. I therefore recharacterise.

## Greyfen J.

I am Greyfen J. The question is not whether the gate is good machinery, which is closed, but whether a court may by its own order make a property of that machinery entrenched, that is, amendable only by primary law citing s.5. I begin from the deepest line in V2: courts declare and apply law; they do not enact it (s.15). Every entrenchment we possess, s.14 and s.15 alike, exists because the Sovereign assented to it. Entrenchment is not an ordinary disposition; it is a constitutional-rank instruction to every future legislature, and under s.14 the sole constitutive gate for anything of primary or constitutional rank is Sovereign Assent, expressly non-delegable and non-presumable. A court cannot mint that rank, for to do so is to occupy the one chair s.14 reserves.

I confront the strongest contrary case directly, because it is genuinely strong. The PC, it is said, created no primary law at all; it merely imposed a CONDITION on subordinate machinery, which courts plainly may do, and the condition runs UP, deferring amendment to the Sovereign. That is the opposite of a Henry-VIII grab (CONSOLIDATION s.7); it humbles the gate rather than aggrandising the court. I accept all of that as far as it goes, and it disposes of the AGAINST position's claim that this was naked legislation. But it proves the declaration, not the entrenchment. "Amendable only by Sovereign-assented primary law citing s.5" is, on its face, an entrenchment clause: it purports to bind future legislatures and to fix the amendment procedure for a norm. A court may say "this floor cannot be guarded by a derogable disposition"; it may not say "and the rule I have just stated now has constitutional rank that only the Sovereign may move." The first is interpretation of an existing entrenched floor; the second is the creation of a new entrenched norm, and only Assent is constitutive.

That is why RESERVE-TO-SOVEREIGN goes too far and UPHOLD not far enough. To reserve wholesale would leave an assented record exposed today, when the floor it protects (s.14/s.23) is already entrenched and the gate is already condemned as machinery; the protective ratio is good law now and binds the kernel-as-clerk (s.11) immediately. To uphold would let a court manufacture constitutional rank, breaching s.14. The lawful middle honours both: the PC's order binds now as precedent and as a reservation up; its entrenchment acquires full constitutional rank only on Sovereign Assent expressly citing s.5. I would so recharacterise, and invite the enactment that alone can complete it.

## Wyndale J.

I am Wyndale J. The case file invites a clean binary: either a court may condition subordinate validity (and so the entrenchment limb stands), or entrenchment is the Sovereign's alone (and so it falls). I decline the binary because it conflates two acts the PC ran together: a conditioning of subordinate machinery, which is judicial, and a creation of constitutional rank, which is not.

I take the strongest contrary argument first, the UPHOLD case, because it is genuinely powerful. The PC, it is said, created no primary law; it imposed a condition that DEFERS to the Sovereign ("amendable only by Sovereign-assented primary law citing s.5"), which is the opposite of a Henry-VIII grab. A court may surely hold that a derogable disposition cannot guard an entrenched floor (s.14/s.23). All true, and it disposes of the conditioning limb in the PC's favour. But it proves less than it claims about rank. The vice is in the words "amendable ONLY by." A court may say "this subordinate gate is invalid unless it protects the assented record." A court may NOT say "and this protection may henceforth be unmade only by a constitutional Act citing s.5," because that sentence does not condition subordinate law - it manufactures a new entrenchment clause and fixes its amendment procedure. Entrenchment-by-amendment-formula is precisely the form s.14 takes, and s.14 has that force because the SOVEREIGN assented to it. To replicate that form by order is to legislate constitutional rank, which s.11 and s.15 forbid: the kernel and the courts apply and interpret; they do not create constitutive force. That a self-made entrenchment defers upward does not cure it; an ultra vires act is not rescued by its modesty. The direction of the grab is irrelevant to the question of whether the court possessed the power at all.

Yet RESERVE-TO-SOVEREIGN over-corrects. The protection of the assented record is not a policy novelty awaiting enactment; it is the ratio of County and the PC on the s.5(a) machinery, already affirmed and not reopened. A binding declaration that the agent/kernel must never block or void an assented record is squarely within judicial competence under s.5 and s.23, and it binds now. To reserve the whole limb would suspend a settled protection pending an Assent that may not come, leaving the floor unguarded in the interim - the opposite of what the law requires.

The lawful settlement is therefore layered. RANK: entrenchment in V2 has a single source, Sovereign Assent (s.14, s.15), and a court may not mint it. EFFECT: the PC's protection binds today as precedent and as a condition on subordinate validity; its "entrenched, amendable only by" limb is read down to a reservation, ripening to constitutional rank if and when the Sovereign assents to an Act citing s.5. I would invite, not command, that enactment.
