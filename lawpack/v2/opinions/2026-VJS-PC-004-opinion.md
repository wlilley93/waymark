# [2026] VJS-PC 4 - Opinion of the V2 Privy Council (Citation and Organisation of Foundational Law)

**Court:** V2 Privy Council (citation system / constitutional structure / V1<->V2 boundary; bench of 3)
**Before:** Adair J, Calloway J, Devereux J
**Cause:** On Lexby's own-motion reference (self-submission duty, [2026] LEXBY-SC 3 s.17(b); first-impression). The Principal's correction was the occasion; the bench decided the law with no access to Lexby's preference.
**Disposition:** Reference ALLOWED. **Unanimous (3-0)** on a HYBRID: adopt the source-keyed citation convention now; leave the entrenched V1 document structurally intact; carry the split prospectively at the restatement step; re-cite the live corpus by a governed clerical pass.
**Registrar:** reduced to record by Lexby (CASE-LAW s.18(4)).

## Ratio (binding)

1. **Cite by source (the convention).** Every citation must resolve through the one citator to exactly one source: **a statute or instrument by its enacting instrument and section** (e.g. "Framework Act s.7"); **case law by its case - its neutral citation** (e.g. "[2026] REALM-SC 10"), never by a section, its binding content being its ratio. Force follows the promulgating organ (the source-of-force test); a pseudo-section over case law resolves to no instrument and is the very thing Bill 16's one-citation-one-source rule excludes.
2. **The hybrid is forced by the category error, not chosen for convenience** (Calloway J). A *section* is the unit of an *enactment*; a *case* is the unit of *adjudication*. V1's `CASE-LAW.md` is two animals in one skin: the **"P" articles** (s.1, s.2, s.3, s.9, s.10, s.13, s.14, s.23) are genuine **Sovereign-enacted constitutional statute** - properly sectioned, merely mis-captioned "case law"; the **"SC" articles** (s.4-s.8, s.11, s.18-s.22) are **case law** whose true address is the case in their source column, the section number being a redundant table-row index.
3. **Case B (re-cutting the V1 document) is rejected; Case A's instinct is adopted in a hybrid.** Re-architecting the entrenched, section-keyed, immutable-digest `CASE-LAW.md` would touch entrenched text or fork the resolvable mapping for no operative gain, against non-disturbance ([2026] VJS-SC 1; [2026] VJS-PC 1). The V1 document **stays as-is** in the archive (estate `v1_archive`, source-only).
4. **Reconciliation with the entrenched machinery.** The entrenched amendment procedure ("cite by section number") and the section-keyed entrenchment list bind **only the genuine P/[constitutional] statute**, where section-keying is correct and is preserved untouched; the convention bites only the case-law half, which never had true sections. No entrenched article is amended; no heightened route is engaged; no id, digest, or lock is disturbed.
5. **The split is carried prospectively.** It is achieved at the **restatement / incorporation step** (Framework Act Schedule 1; a future V3 restatement), where each principle is re-expressed under a source-correct citation. The clean separation - a sectioned Sovereign-enacted constitution and a body of case law cited by case - is thereby inherited by V3 without rewriting an entrenched byte now.
6. **Scope and manner: a governed clerical pass, not adoption.** Re-citing the **live V2 corpus only** (the principles digest / Schedule 1, the Framework Act body, the Transition Doctrine, filed-opinion source tags) - mapping each "CASE-LAW s.X" to its true resolver (an SC article to its case from the source column; a P article to its instrument + section) - changes no legal meaning (Framework Act s.5; Bill 14 s.24), is verifiable by diff, follows the [2026] VJS-PC 3 transcription discipline (read off the source column, nothing fabricated), and **needs no fresh adoption**. The case<->section mapping is lodged as a resolvable register; principle 20's gate is extended to **fail closed on a pseudo-section over an SC-sourced article** going forward.

## The opinions, in brief
- **Adair J** (least-disturbance): a labelling defect, not a defect of force; adopt the convention; resolve the category by reading the existing source column (classification, not re-cutting); the split belongs at the V2 restatement; no heightened route engaged; re-citation is a meaning-preserving clerical conformance pass.
- **Calloway J** (the category analysis; controlling): a section is the unit of an enactment, a case the unit of adjudication; the hybrid is *forced* - the constitution keeps sections because it is statute, case law sheds them because it never had any; convention adopted now, split carried at the restatement step, the V1 archive untouched; lodge the case<->section mapping as a register.
- **Devereux J** (operability/citator): a citation is good only if it resolves through the one citator to exactly one source; case law already resolves by its REALM neutral citation, so "CASE-LAW s.X" over an SC article is a redundant non-resolving pseudo-handle; clerical re-citation, enforced prospectively by extending the principle-20 gate to fail closed on a pseudo-section over an SC-sourced article.

## Disposal
Unanimous (3-0). Adopt the source-keyed convention; preserve the entrenched V1 document intact in the archive; carry the split at the restatement step; re-cite the live V2 corpus by a governed, meaning-preserving clerical pass (no adoption), lodging the case<->section mapping as a register and extending the principle-20 citation gate to fail closed on a pseudo-section over a court-made article. Recorded by Lexby as registrar.
