## Tindale J - HYBRID

**Ratio.** The canon is the realm's shared higher law that independent subscribers pin by digest, so it must remain a separate, public, AGPL repository with no inbound dependency and no product code; the private MIT product/engine/policy layers may consolidate into a "governance house" by court order, but only outside the canon and only if every subscriber can keep pinning the exact piece it vendors by sha256 without taking the whole tree.

**Reasoning.**

From the institutional/precedent lens the controlling fact is the dependency direction, and I verified it in-repo: the lattice is strictly acyclic and the canon is the root. Canon depends on nothing (no `kern` in its Cargo.lock; its crates do not import kern); kern depends on nothing; VPS->canon; the subscriber's >kern+canon+VPS. The canon is therefore the shared higher law that multiple INDEPENDENT subscribers pin, not a leaf in anyone's product. the subscriber proves this is live, not theoretical: it vendors kern at sha256 015ad8..., the lawpack at sha256 14cdb3..., and the VPS policypack, each as an unmodified, drift-gated in-tree copy (regular blobs, not a submodule). Reading A (full consolidate) is institutionally wrong precisely here. Putting the canon inside a product-ish monorepo would (a) entangle the law's clock with product releases, (b) force a subscriber to vendor a giant tree to get the one piece it pins, defeating the clean-shared-reference value Reading A claims to keep, and (c) put AGPL law and MIT product code under one roof, risking the licence/exposure bleed that separate repos enforce for free. I verified the boundary is real and load-bearing: canon is PUBLIC/AGPL-3.0, kern and VPS are PRIVATE/MIT, and the only lawful cross-consumption today runs through the Principal's recorded embed-point grant (the 0366 note: vendored vjs-core stamped Apache-2.0 OR AGPL, the subscriber's allow-list permissive-only). Merging would dissolve the natural enforcement that the binding record relies on.

But Reading B's reflex ("keep everything separate, full stop") over-reads the law and ignores a real cost: assembly friction across engine + policy-template + products is genuine and is private-layer-only. REALM-PC-19's ratio (verified in the valid-law digest at line 222 and the embedded-corporate-jurisdiction submission) is narrower than "never consolidate": superrepo/topology changes require a court order, AND, crucially, the same submission already records the binding distinction that a new independent subscriber repo is NOT a change to the canonical superrepo topology. So the law does not forbid a governance house; it forbids doing it without an order and forbids touching the canon's topology by side effect. That maps cleanly onto a hybrid: keep the canon separate, public, and root-of-the-lattice as the clean shared reference; permit the private MIT layers to consolidate into a house by a future order, fenced by conditions that hold every institutional invariant constant.

The conditions are chosen to keep the binding record stable and the law a clean shared reference. The canon's separateness, publicness, AGPL stamp, and zero-inbound-dependency are non-negotiable because they are what make the record citable and pin-able. Subscriber-pinnability is preserved as a hard invariant so no consolidation can coarsen the unit a subscriber vendors (the the subscriber kern.pin and lawpack.lock must keep working unchanged). kern and vjs-core stay un-merged: I confirmed they are orthogonal (different licences, specs, and clocks; neither imports the other), so reconciling them is a separate first-impression matter, not a free rider on this structural order. REALM-SC-11 (digest-pinned copy, no live egress) is restated so consolidation cannot smuggle in a live dependency. Finally, because the value Reading A legitimately wants is a coherent deployment bundle, I require the house, if formed, to publish a binding deployment-bundle spec recording the exact canon digest, kern pin, and policypack version per release, so the bundle's provenance against the public binding record stays auditable. Net: the law stays one clean public shared root; the products get their house, lawfully and only by a further order under REALM-PC-19.

**Conditions.**

- The canon (vibe-justice-system) stays its own repository: PUBLIC, AGPL-3.0, the binding record, with zero inbound dependency. It must never become a package inside a product-ish monorepo.
- No consolidation may collapse the public(AGPL)/private(MIT) boundary. The canon's AGPL copyleft and the private layers' permissive supply-chain posture must stay separated at the repo boundary; the Principal's embed-point licence grant (Apache-2.0 OR AGPL for the vendored vjs-core copy) governs any cross-licence consumption and must be recorded in the consuming repo's lock.
- Any consolidation of the private layers (engine + policy-template + products) into a governance house is itself a superrepo/topology change and requires a fresh court order under REALM-PC-19; this opinion authorises the SHAPE, not the act. A new independent subscriber repo remains free (it is not a topology change per the embedded-corporate-jurisdiction holding), but folding existing separate repos together is.
- Subscriber-pinnability is preserved as a hard invariant: the subscriber and any future subscriber must be able to vendor exactly one piece (kern, the lawpack, a policypack) by sha256, unmodified and drift-gated, without vendoring a giant unrelated tree. If a monorepo would force a subscriber to pin a coarser unit than today, that part stays separate.
- kern and vjs-core are NOT force-merged. They are orthogonal (neither imports the other; different clocks, different licences, different specs). Any future reconciliation is a separate first-impression question on its own merits, not a consequence of this structural order.
- REALM-SC-11 is preserved: subscribers carry a digest-pinned COPY of the law with no live egress. Consolidation must not introduce a live dependency on the canon for any subscriber or product.
- If the private layers consolidate, the governance house must publish a binding deployment-bundle spec recording, per release, the exact canon-lawpack digest, kern pin, and policypack version it assembles, so the bundle's provenance against the public binding record stays auditable.

## Rowanne J - HYBRID

**Ratio.** The canon is shared higher law that more than one independent subscriber pins by sha256, so it must stay a separate, public, minimally-vendorable repo; but the operator's real assembly pain (the deployment unit) is solved not by a monorepo and not by leaving everything loose, but by a binding, machine-checkable deployment-bundle manifest that names each repo, its sha256 pin, and its licence, so a builder can reproduce the house from one file without entangling the four independent clocks.

**Reasoning.**

From the operator's lens, the question is not abstract elegance but: can a builder look at one thing and reproduce the deployment unit (policy house + kernel + carried canon) without ceremony? Reading A (consolidate) correctly diagnoses the pain - assembly friction and a non-obvious deployment unit - but misprescribes the cure. A monorepo would solve discoverability while creating four new operator problems: it entangles four independent release clocks (the very thing sha256 pinning exists to decouple), forces every subscriber to vendor a giant tree to get one leaf, and co-mingles an AGPL public repo with MIT private trees at exactly the boundary where a slip leaks licence or source. The evidence is unambiguous that the pieces are already clean leaves: canon->nothing, kern->nothing, VPS->canon, the subscriber's >kern+canon+VPS, no cycles, two engines that genuinely never import each other. A monorepo would take four well-factored, independently-pinnable components and braid them, and the operator would feel that braid on every bump.

Reading B (keep separate) is right about the structural constraints - the canon is shared higher law that multiple independent subscribers pin, so it cannot live inside a product monorepo, and the licence boundary is enforced for free by separate repos - but it under-delivers on the operator's actual complaint. 'Keep separate' as-is leaves the deployment unit implicit: a builder has to know, out of band, which four repos at which four digests compose the house. That is precisely the assembly-friction tax Reading A was reacting to. The pragmatic answer is the hybrid the file already flagged: keep the canon separate and public; keep kern separate; do NOT force-merge the engines; and pay down the friction with the one artifact that makes the house obvious and reproducible - a binding deployment-bundle manifest. The infrastructure for this already exists and is live: .vjs/lawpack.lock in both VPS and the subscriber is exactly this shape (lawpack id + sha256 digest + source_commit + adoption_mode + basis). Lifting that pattern to a house-level bundle.lock that names every component, its pin, and its licence, with a single fail-closed `bundle verify`, gives the operator the single home and the reproducible assembly Reading A wanted, without the entanglement Reading B feared.

On the constraints: REALM-PC-19 requires a court order for topology change - this ruling IS that order, but a tightly scoped one: it authorises the bundle-spec topology and a permitted product-layer house (engine-adjacent product code, policy-template, products as an optional packages monorepo that pins canon and kern as external vendored copies), and it expressly withholds authority for anything heavier (merging histories, re-homing the canon, collapsing the two engines), each of which would need its own order on its own facts. The the subscriber subscriber is protected by the minimal-vendor-by-component rule: it keeps its three existing per-component locks; the bundle manifest is additive, never a forced mega-vendor. The AGPL/MIT boundary stays enforced by repo separation plus an explicit per-component licence field that `bundle verify` checks fail-closed, so the public law and the private engine never bleed into one distribution boundary by accident. That is the arrangement a builder can actually assemble and ship: four honest leaves, one lockfile that composes them, one verify command that proves the house.

**Conditions.**

- The canon (vibe-justice-system) stays a SEPARATE, public, AGPL-3.0 repo. It is the shared higher law that the subscriber and future products pin by sha256; it must remain small and cheaply vendorable as a leaf (canon->nothing). It MUST NOT be absorbed into any product-ish or MIT monorepo.
- kern stays a SEPARATE, private, MIT repo. It is jurisdiction-neutral, pinned by spec/digest, and shares zero code with the canon's vjs-core (verified: neither imports the other). Do not force-merge the two engines; reconciliation, if ever, is a later first-impression reference on its own facts, not a side effect of consolidation.
- A binding DEPLOYMENT-BUNDLE SPEC is enacted as the actual deliverable of this reference. It is one machine-readable manifest (e.g. bundle.lock) listing, for the governance house, each component repo + its sha256 digest + source_commit + licence (AGPL vs MIT) + adoption_mode (vendored-read-only). It reuses the existing .vjs/lawpack.lock shape already live in VPS and the subscriber, and a single `bundle verify` reproduces and hash-checks the whole house fail-closed. This is what gives the operator one obvious, reproducible deployment unit without a monorepo.
- The manifest MUST carry an explicit licence field per component and `bundle verify` MUST fail closed if an AGPL component is vendored into an MIT distribution boundary without the AGPL terms travelling with it. The public(AGPL)/private(MIT) split stays enforced by repo boundary plus this gate; do not co-mingle AGPL canon source into the MIT trees.
- A 'house' consolidation IS permitted, but only for the leaf-free product layer that already depends downward: engine-adjacent product code, policy-template, and products MAY be arranged as a packages monorepo at the operator's discretion (reversible, low blast radius) PROVIDED it pins the canon and kern as external sha256-locked vendored copies, never as in-tree forks, and never pulls the canon into it.
- REALM-PC-19 (superrepo/topology change requires a court order) is SATISFIED by this ruling as the order for the bundle-spec topology and the permitted product-house arrangement. Any FURTHER physical move - merging repo histories, re-homing the canon, or collapsing kern and vjs-core - is a fresh topology change requiring its own court order and is NOT authorised here.
- Subscriber continuity is preserved: the subscriber keeps vendoring kern + canon-lawpack + VPS policypack by its existing per-component sha256 locks (verified live: kernel/, lawpack/, vps/ each with their own lock). No subscriber is forced to vendor a giant tree to obtain one piece; minimal-vendor-by-component is the rule, and the bundle manifest is additive over it.
- The deployment-bundle spec MUST be published in the public canon (it governs how the shared law is assembled) while component digests for private repos may resolve to private sources; publishing the spec does not expose private source, only the pin metadata.

## Marchmont J - HYBRID

**Ratio.** The canon (public, AGPL) is shared higher law referenced by multiple independent sha-pinned subscribers and must remain a separate public repository; consolidating it into any product-ish house would entangle versioning, force a live subscriber to vendor a giant tree for one piece, and collapse the AGPL/MIT firewall that separation enforces by construction. A "house" grouping of engine + policy-template + products is permissible ONLY as a reversible, additive convenience that preserves each piece as an independently sha-pinnable unit and never republishes an AGPL tree alongside MIT trees, because REALM-PC-19 forbids topology change absent a court order and CC-the subscriber 56 C14 reserves any restructure touching the live subscriber's pinned engine to a further sitting.

**Reasoning.**

From the federation/risk lens the asymmetry of regret is stark, so I favour the lower-regret path. Reading B's core insight is correct on the verified facts: the canon is not a product, it is shared higher law with multiple independent subscribers (the subscriber today, future products tomorrow) that pin it by sha and consume it as a vendored, digest-verified copy under REALM-SC-11 (no live egress of the law) and the VJS-PC 10 pin discipline. A consolidated single 'governance house' that swallowed the canon would entangle four independent versioning clocks, force every subscriber to vendor a giant tree to obtain one pinned crate or lawpack, and - most dangerously - dissolve the AGPL/MIT firewall that today is enforced for free by repository separation. That firewall is not theoretical: the repo record shows it is already an active, hand-managed risk (the subscriber's permissive-only deny.toml plus a deliberate Principal copyright re-grant re-stamping the AGPL vjs-core at the embed point). A merge that put AGPL canon source co-resident with MIT engine/policy/product trees in one published repo is exactly the exposure-bleed scenario that re-grant exists to prevent; the cost of getting that wrong on a live, possibly-distributed downstream is a licence-compliance failure, not a refactor. That is high-regret and hard to reverse. Reading A's genuine merits - operator mental model, assembly friction, one deployment home - are real but they are about ASSEMBLY, not OWNERSHIP, and can be captured almost entirely by a binding deployment-bundle/manifest spec that pins each repo by sha without relocating a single tree. That is the low-blast path that delivers most of A's value while preserving all of B's safety. Hence the hybrid: canon stays separate and public as the shared law (non-negotiable); a private, MIT-only 'house' for engine + policy-template + products is permissible in principle but only behind a court order under REALM-PC-19 and a further CC-the subscriber 56 C14 sitting at which the live subscriber is heard, and only if it preserves per-piece sha pinning, the licence firewall, and a reversible split-back path. I do not force-merge the two engines: they are orthogonal by the verified dependency graph and reconciling them is its own first-impression question, not a restructure side effect. Procedurally I emphasise that REALM-PC-19 and CC-the subscriber 56 C14 mean I cannot and do not order the physical restructure here; I authorise the hybrid in principle and the deployment-bundle spec now (decisive, reversible, within execution-step latitude), and route the concrete topology move back for an implementation order with the subscriber joined - the lowest-regret sequencing for a live downstream pinned by sha.

**Conditions.**

- CANON STAYS SEPARATE AND PUBLIC: the canon (vibe-justice-system, AGPL-3.0) is not folded into any monorepo; it remains the standalone, public, shared higher law that subscribers reference. It is the one tree with multiple independent downstreams and the one AGPL surface; it cannot live inside a private product house.
- LICENCE FIREWALL IS NON-NEGOTIABLE: no restructure may place an AGPL tree and an MIT tree in a single published repository such that copyleft reaches a permissive binary. The existing posture (the subscriber deny.toml permissive-only allow-list; the Principal re-grant re-stamping vendored vjs-core 'Apache-2.0 OR AGPL-3.0-only' at the embed point) is the floor and must survive any move. If a house is built, it is private and MIT-only; the AGPL canon is consumed by vendored, re-stamped, pinned copy, never by co-resident source.
- SUBSCRIBER PINS ARE PRESERVED BYTE-FOR-BYTE: any 'house' must keep kern, the canon lawpack, and the policypack each independently addressable so the subscriber's sha256 pins (vps/kern.pin, the lawpack digest under VJS-PC 10 / REALM-SC-11) resolve to the same content. A subscriber must never be forced to vendor a giant tree to obtain one pinned piece. If a single repo cannot guarantee per-piece pinning without tree-size bleed, the house is not built.
- NO FORCED ENGINE MERGE: vjs-core (the canon's first-generation machinery) and kern (the neutral SPEC-KERNEL-002 core) are orthogonal, solve different problems, and neither imports the other. They are NOT reconciled or force-merged under cover of a restructure. Engine reconciliation is a separate first-impression question on its own record, not a side effect of housing.
- COURT ORDER IS A PRECONDITION, NOT A FORMALITY: REALM-PC-19 requires a court order before any superrepo/topology change is implemented, and CC-the subscriber 56 C14 expressly reserves 'standalone extraction of kern' and any change to the subscriber's pinned engine to a further sitting. This opinion authorises the hybrid IN PRINCIPLE only; the concrete restructure returns for an implementation order that the subscriber (the subscriber) is heard on, with the binding deployment-bundle spec below tabled.
- ENACT A DEPLOYMENT-BUNDLE SPEC INSTEAD OF MOVING REPOS FIRST: the operator's real need (one home for the assembly, cut assembly friction) is met by a binding deployment-bundle / manifest spec that names each repo + pinned sha and assembles the bundle, WITHOUT relocating the trees. This delivers Reading A's benefit at near-zero blast radius and is the lower-regret first step; only if it proves insufficient does a physical house come back to court.
- REVERSIBILITY AND PIN-CONTINUITY ON ANY MOVE: if a house is later ordered, it ships with a documented split-back path and a migration that re-points every live pin in one audited commit (mirroring the subscriber's own 'relocation salvage' burden), so a wrong restructure is cheaply reversible on the live downstream.
